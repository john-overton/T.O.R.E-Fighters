# v0.2.0: Multiplayer and the Living Battlefield (Working Title)

**Released:** TBD · **Channel:** Pre-release · **Tag:** [v0.2.0](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.0)

Version 0.2.0 is the Multiplayer milestone. Build a Quick Mission with friends,
fly co-op or PvP, then return to the lobby for another round. Single player gains
the same ground targets, strike weapons, aircraft and richer world. Try a
night strike on a defended target, or work the AC-130's sensor console while
its autopilot flies the orbit.

## Getting started

1. Bring your own Fighters Anthology installation or disc 1, as before. If your
   import is missing aircraft or shared data, use **Pref > Re-import media**.
2. Open **Multi > Direct Connection** for a local game or a typed address, or
   **Multi > Internet Lobby** to find an online game. Host with **New**, or pick
   a game and press **Join**. Take a slot, choose a loadout and mark **Ready**.
3. Use the same T.O.R.E build as your host. This release uses **protocol 63**:
   older network versions cannot join it. New live recordings use **replay
   format 4**; this version still plays older recordings in formats 1, 2 and 3.
   Older game builds cannot read the new format 4 recordings.

See the [multiplayer guide](../MULTIPLAYER.md) for joining and hosting, including
Internet Lobby connectivity and its optional anonymous statistics.

## Highlights

### Multiplayer

- **Up to 30 humans** can fly a Quick Mission together or on opposing sides.
  AI can fill the remaining slots, or the King can turn AI aircraft off.
- The **King** chooses the mission and rules, with loadouts and readiness in
  the shared lobby. Players can inspect the mission, chat and return to the
  lobby after flying. A 10-second Force Start countdown can start unready slot
  holders, while still respecting minimum-player and AI-off requirements.
- PvP has coloured Bluefor and Redfor slots, side restrictions and Autobalance.
  Revival rules control lives, delay and placement, with optional AI respawn.
- **Player Choice** wings let each human pick an aircraft. When revival is
  enabled, the waiting player can prepare a new loadout and, in a Choice slot,
  another aircraft for the next spawn.
- Join a flight in progress, watch as an observer, or rejoin your reserved
  aircraft after a dropped connection. Eligible standby players can take over
  hosting when the host leaves or is lost.
- The flight data link shares contacts, locks and assignments. Human wingmen
  receive orders and can answer with radio replies; text chat has separate
  receivers for the whole game, a side, a wing or a target.
- The Internet Lobby lists games and tries direct connections, router port
  forwarding, connection through routers and a relay when needed. Direct
  Connection still works without the public list server.
- A **dedicated server** can run without a window on Linux, Windows or macOS.
  Operators can rotate maps and missions in order or shuffled, set rules per
  mission and keep players connected between rounds. See the
  [server guide](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md).

### Air-to-ground

- Find and designate ground contacts on the **A-G radar** page. Use targeting
  sights and pods to track a target or pin a point on the ground.
- Unguided bombs have **CCIP**, a mark for their predicted impact point, and a
  level-bombing guide with hold-to-release. Rocket sights and low-altitude,
  fragmentation and overflight warnings support the attack run.
- Laser designation and **buddy lasing** guide laser bombs to your own or a
  friendly aircraft's spot. Guided bombs include GBU-12, GBU-16 and GBU-38,
  alongside the other supported laser and satellite-guided stores.
- Mavericks, HARM and other supported ground missiles have their targeting
  cues. Terrain can mask a target or laser line, and weapon seeker views let
  you follow the attack from the weapon's nose.
- The Pave Spike targeting pod can be loaded on a compatible station. Load
  Ordnance explains the aircraft's sensor equipment and warns when laser bombs
  need a friendly designator.
- Strike wings and ordered wingmen attack targets their stores can hit, with
  bombing, rockets, guided weapons, strafing and laser support. The same
  air-to-ground weapons and displays work online. See the
  [air-to-ground specification](../spec/air-to-ground.md).

### The surface world

- Quick Mission offers **ground targets and air defenses in all 16 theaters**,
  with target objectives and debrief results. SAMs and guns can also defend
  enemy territory when no ground target is selected.
- SAM batteries, anti-aircraft guns and flak engage aircraft. Nearby flak guns
  coordinate their opening barrage, and supply trucks replenish defenses.
- Moving columns, tanks, infantry, ships and opposing ground units bring the
  battlefield to life. Turrets and guns aim at their targets; parked aircraft
  can be attacked on airfields and carrier decks.
- **Carriers** appear with their hulls, islands, deck equipment, crew and
  defenses. They are surface targets in this release, with full carrier flight
  operations still to come.
- Destroyed buildings and vehicles leave ruins or wrecks. Buildings, including
  city-fill buildings, burn for the mission, and weapon craters and gun marks
  remain on the terrain. See the [surface world](../spec/surface-defenses.md).

### AC-130 gunship and sensor console

- Aim and link the AC-130U's **25 mm, 40 mm and 105 mm guns**. Each gun keeps its
  own ammunition and firing rate; the 105 mm also damages nearby soft targets.
- Open the full-screen **sensor console** and let the autopilot fly the gun
  orbit. Search, track or pin a target, slew and zoom the sensor, and work the
  guns with keyboard, mouse or gamepad controls.
- Switch between white-hot, black-hot, TV and night-vision pictures. The console
  includes a ground radar map, aiming marks, gun information and a diagram
  showing where the sensor is pointing. See the
  [console guide](../spec/target-window.md#sensor-console).

### Rotorcraft and VTOL

- Fly the AH-64, Mi-24 and CH-47 with **rotor physics**, collective and cyclic
  controls, rotor-speed and torque indications, trim and hover hold. Ground
  effect, autorotation and hazardous rotor states are modelled.
- Convert the V-22 between helicopter and airplane flight. The AV-8B and
  Yak-141 use vectored thrust for vertical flight within their weight limits.
- Helicopters and VTOL aircraft can fly as AI wingmen or enemies, take off and
  land vertically, and carry out their supported attack roles.
- Land suitable powered-lift aircraft on open ground or flat building tops
  within their speed, sink and slope limits. Rotor fuel use follows engine
  load, with corrected V-22 and CH-47 fuel capacities.
- Player-flyable helicopters are a **T.O.R.E addition**. These flight models
  include fitted handling, with further pilot testing and tuning still needed.
  See [powered-lift flight](../spec/powered-lift-flight.md).

## Improvements

### Aircraft and stores on real stations

- The roster adds **23 aircraft**, including Phantoms, F-15C, F-16C, A-10,
  transports, radar aircraft, airliners, the gunship and powered-lift aircraft.
  There are 36 reviewed retail aircraft plus the F/A-XX concept; see the
  [aircraft roster](../spec/aircraft-variety.md).
- Weapons and tanks are drawn at their corrected sizes on aircraft stations.
  Pylons, rails and racks carry them, and stores leave from where they are drawn
  in live flights, online and in replays.
- Aircraft-specific layouts include the F-14's tunnel Phoenixes and nacelle
  tanks, Flanker missile stations, F-15C tank and bomb options, F-16C wingtip
  AIM-120s, F-22 external carriers and MiG-29 and Rafale C centreline tanks.
- **Jettison** tanks or the selected external store. Dropped stores are inert,
  remove their weight and fuel, and leave small impact marks. External stores
  also increase the aircraft's radar signature.
- Load Ordnance lists stations by their place on the aircraft and keeps the
  current occupant visible. See [stores on pylons](../spec/stores-on-pylons.md).

### Sky and lighting

- Dawn and dusk change smoothly, with a moving moon and a turning starfield.
  Stars fade as daylight returns, and terrain follows the low sun's lighting.
- Fires and explosions glow at night and light nearby surfaces. Red and green
  tracers stay visible farther away, with a night glow.
- Cockpit paint and bezels darken with daylight and weather while displays
  remain lit. **Red flood lights**, instrument brightness and independent HUD
  brightness and colour settings are saved between flights.
- Sun glare and lens flare disappear behind terrain, objects and aircraft.
  See [cockpit lighting](../spec/cockpit-lighting.md).

### World

- **Redrawn airports** use real-scale runways, taxiways and aprons, with runway,
  approach, taxiway and approach-slope lights. Paired airport tiles become one
  long-runway field where appropriate.
- City fill adds buildings by default. Theater vegetation adds trees, bushes,
  palms and rocks with original T.O.R.E artwork, plus grass and smoother terrain
  texture transitions.
- Ground objects cast shadows, with tree shadows at higher quality settings.
  Graphics options control vegetation, grass, shadows and their draw distances.
- **Ground pads** level terrain under flat and large fixed footprints. The
  simulation and renderer use the same surface, so those sites sit on their
  ground consistently. See [objects and grounding](../formats/objects-and-shapes.md).

### Sound and blast effects

- Distant **explosions arrive at the speed of sound**, after their flash.
- Hear other aircraft's guns and weapon releases, and surface missile launches,
  in live play, multiplayer and replays, with distance and cockpit muffling.
- Large blasts throw out shockwave effects: brief vapor in humid air, lingering
  dust in dry theaters and spray over water. These visual effects are a
  T.O.R.E addition. See [explosions](../spec/explosions.md) and
  [weapon sound](../spec/sound.md).

### Replays

- Network sessions convert to replays after they end and appear in the Replays
  screen. Recordings include surface movement, destruction, stores and
  air-to-ground events.
- The **HUD camera** has an old video-tape look. Sensor View shows the recorded
  aircraft's targeting sight, including the AC-130 console, with direct picture
  mode selection.
- New live recordings sample HUD and console displays at **24 Hz**, preserving
  aiming marks, ground targets, radar contacts and sensor limits. The HUD tape
  picture also runs at 24 frames a second while playback controls stay responsive.
- The selected-object banner shows speed, altitude, heading, action and target.
  Watch weapons in flight or follow moving surface units with the object camera.
- Old recordings still play, with simpler display reconstruction when the older
  file lacks the new samples. See [mission replays](../REPLAYS.md).

### Performance

- CPU work can spread across worker threads when needed. Heavy fights, network
  hosting, city loading, cockpit drawing, vegetation and shadows have had
  optimization passes while simulation stays at **120 Hz**.
- **Frame rate** offers 60, 100, 200 or V-sync, with V-sync as the default.
  An optional counter shows frame rate and timing.
- Mission preparation runs behind a loading screen. Idle menus and hidden
  windows do less work; a minimized multiplayer game keeps its session running.
  See [graphics settings](../spec/graphics-options.md).

## Fixes

- Joining players and hosts keep their connections alive during long mission
  builds. A dropped Player Choice pilot keeps the chosen aircraft for rejoin.
- Host migration flushes waiting simulation updates so a killed host does not
  leave the standby missing the last completed ticks.
- Thin lines on surface models no longer inflate their hit boxes and spoil
  Maverick attacks. Ground marks follow slopes without covering vehicles.
- AI aircraft start with the fuel in their default external tanks. They keep
  their weapons when entering a fight instead of discarding the strike load.
- AI helicopters keep their rotors loaded during fast descents, and the V-22
  follows rising terrain more safely. Heavy aircraft retain the pull needed
  to recover from a fast descent.
- A drop-launched missile no longer fuzes on its own launcher before clearing
  it. Active-radar missiles use the correct Fox three call, and player launch
  range cues are advisory.
- Replays preserve burning city smoke when seeking and show loaded stores even
  when they were never fired. Vegetation no longer has white transparency
  fringes on thin branches.

## Known limitations

- **Campaigns, standalone missions and full carrier operations are still ahead.**
  This milestone centres on Quick Missions, including multiplayer.
- **Some weapons and aircraft details remain incomplete.** The
  [feature matrix](../features.md) records unsupported stores, fitted handling
  and outstanding animation and flight checks.
- **Validation is broader than before, but incomplete.** Earlier multiplayer
  builds were tried on Linux, Windows and macOS; the latest host migration,
  threading and expanded aircraft still need wider platform and multi-machine
  checks. This draft is based on committed features and recorded evidence,
  not a new end-to-end playtest of every feature.
- **Older recordings lack newer cockpit samples.** They remain watchable, but
  cannot recover information their original build never recorded. Network
  capture conversion also has display limits listed in the
  [replay guide](../REPLAYS.md#network-flights).
- **Retail comparison is unavailable.** Fitted and opinionated components are
  documented; this release does not establish exact parity with the original.
