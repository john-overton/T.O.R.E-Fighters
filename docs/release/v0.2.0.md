# v0.2.0: Wake up, dear, multiplayer is here!

**Released:** TBD · **Channel:** Pre-release · **Tag:** [v0.2.0](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.0)

Version 0.2.0 is **Milestone 2: multiplayer**, and the biggest update since the
first build. Build a Quick Mission, open it to friends on your network or the
internet, and fly it together against the AI or against each other, then come
back to the lobby for another round. Single player gains just as much: ground
targets defended by SAMs, guns and flak in all 16 theaters, a full set of
air-to-ground weapons and sights, the AC-130 gunship with its sensor console,
23 more aircraft including helicopters, the V-22 and the Harrier, and a world
with real-scale airports, cities, trees, dawn and dusk. To see most of it at
once, fly a dusk strike on a defended target in the A-10 or the F-16C, then
watch it back through the HUD camera.

> **Everyone in a multiplayer game needs this exact release.** Builds play
> together only when their network protocol matches, so 0.2.0 cannot play with
> an older or newer build. The protocol number is under
> [Getting started](#getting-started).

## Getting started

1. Download the package for your platform from the
   [release page](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.0)
   and install it over the previous version.
2. **Your media is imported again, once.** This release keeps more of your
   copy: the Quick Mission ground target data and the multiplayer screens' art.
   The game re-imports on its own from the copy it remembers; if it cannot find
   it, point it at your Fighters Anthology again. **Pref > Re-import media**
   works at any time.
3. **Multiplayer needs the same build on every machine.** This release uses
   **network protocol 67**. A game on another protocol shows as another build
   and cannot be joined. On the **Multi** menu, **Direct Connection...** opens the
   **Direct Network Lounge**, which finds games on your local network and joins
   a typed address, and **Internet...** opens the **Internet Lounge**, which
   lists public games.
   **New** in either one hosts a game. The [multiplayer guide](../MULTIPLAYER.md)
   covers the rest.
4. **New recordings use replay format 4.** This build still plays every older
   recording. Builds limited to formats 2 or 3 cannot open format 4.
   Feedback uses ordinary event fields that other format-4 readers can decode.
5. **Dedicated servers** have their own downloads on this release:
   `tore-server-0.2.0-windows-x86_64.zip`, `-macos-arm64.tar.gz`,
   `-macos-x86_64.tar.gz` and `-linux-x86_64.tar.gz`, each with a sample
   configuration and mission. The game is not needed on the server machine,
   only your own Fighters Anthology to import. Follow the
   [server guide](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md#downloading-it). You do not need one
   to play: any player can host from the game.

## Highlights

### Multiplayer

- **Up to 30 human pilots in one Quick Mission**: 30 against each other in
  PvP, or 15 together against the AI in co-op. The original allowed 8. The AI
  flies every slot nobody takes, and takes over at once when a player drops.
- **Host from the game or join one.** **New** hosts the Quick Mission you have
  built. The game asks your router to forward its port (UPnP, NAT-PMP or PCP;
  a switch in **Options** turns this off), and the Internet Lounge connects
  players through routers, falling back to a relay on the list server when no
  direct path works.
- **The lobby.** Take a slot, arm your own aircraft with **Loadout**, press
  **Ready**, and the King (the hosting player) presses **Fly**. Everyone can
  read the mission on **Mission...**; only the King changes it.
  **Settings...** holds the King's rules on four pages (Game, Revival, Scoring
  and Realism), **Players...** passes the crown or kicks, and a hint above the
  buttons says what to do next. Each player's system (Windows, macOS or Linux)
  shows beside their name.
- **Force Start.** When some players are ready and others are not, the King's
  Fly becomes **Force Start**: a 10 second countdown, after which the players
  who are still not ready fly with the loadout they have.
- **PvP sides.** Blue and red slots, **Bluefor** and **Redfor** boxes to pick a
  side, and a Sides rule: Free, Locked once flown, or Balanced, where
  **Autobalance** seats each player on the side with fewer humans.
- **Revival and respawn.** The original's revival rules (lives, delay,
  distance from the battle and weapons), with distances out to 150 nm. Lost AI
  aircraft can respawn too. **Player Choice** wings let each pilot pick their
  own aircraft, and while you wait to fly again you can change your loadout, or
  your aircraft in a Choice slot. **Loadout...** and **Aircraft...** stay
  available from Escape throughout the wait, including after the delay.
  Done saves your choices; close the menus and press **Enter** or **controller
  A** when ready to respawn. There is no automatic respawn. The King can also
  turn AI aircraft off.
- **Join a mission in flight** by taking over an AI aircraft, or press
  **Watch** to follow the fight live in the replay viewer and scrub back up to
  10 minutes. If you leave your game at a menu or in the background for 5
  minutes, the AI flies your aircraft until you choose **Take Back Flight** on
  the Escape menu.
- **The game survives losing its host.** Another player's game takes over
  hosting and everyone flies on about 2 to 3.5 seconds later, with missiles in
  flight and scores intact. A player who drops can rejoin their kept aircraft.
- **Talk and fight as a team.** The **~** key opens chat to everyone, your side,
  your wing or your target, with the original's quick messages on F1 to F12.
  The flight data link shares contacts and locks across your flight:
  **Alt+A** sorts each wingman onto a different bandit with a spoken
  assignment call, **Alt+N** listens to the other flights, and human wingmen
  answer orders with **Alt+Shift+E**, W, B and H (Engaging, Winchester, Bingo
  fuel, Need help).
- **Scores.** Press **K** in flight for the score board. A kill limit can end
  a PvP mission, and the debrief adds SCORES and RESULTS pages with every aircraft's
  pilot, fate and kills.
- **Everything else flies online too**: ground targets and their defenses,
  every air-to-ground weapon and sight, buddy lasing between players, the
  AC-130 console, and every aircraft's stores emptying as they are fired.
- **Lounges and lobbies.** Both lounges and the lobby have a **?** menu with
  **Return to Main Menu** and **Exit**, which disconnect cleanly, and their
  games and players lists scroll with the red scroll bar. The lobby is titled
  **Internet Game Lobby** or **Direct Network Game Lobby**. Changes on the Settings
  pages, Realism included, apply once when you close Settings (Close or F10);
  Escape discards them. **Minimum players** in Settings > Revival works with
  AI on or off. Empty AI-off slots stay available for late joins; Force Start
  works once the ready-player minimum is met.
- **Dedicated servers** run with no window on Linux, Windows and macOS, rotate
  through missions in order or shuffled, can wait for a minimum number of ready
  players, and keep everyone connected between rounds. See the
  [server guide](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md).
- **Dedicated recording and feedback are opt-in.** Server replay retention
  keeps the newest unflagged missions and every flagged mission indefinitely.
  Logs rotate with their recordings. See [server settings](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md#recording-and-feedback).
- Your multiplayer flights are saved as replays when they end, in the Replays
  screen like any other. Listing or joining a game through the Internet Lounge
  sends anonymous statistics, on by default and switched off in **Options**;
  the [README](../../README.md#online-play-and-anonymous-statistics) lists
  exactly what is sent.


### Air-to-ground weapons

- **Find the target.** **Ctrl+R** switches the radar to its air-to-ground page,
  and **T**, **Shift+T**, **Enter** or a click on the scope designates ground
  objects. Aircraft with a laser or an infrared sensor get a **targeting
  sight**: slew it with **Alt+arrows**, zoom, pin a point on the ground with
  **Shift+Backslash** or track a vehicle, and the target window becomes a
  targeting page.
- **Bomb sights.** The HUD has a CCIP pipper for unguided bombs, marking where
  the bomb meets the terrain, a level bombing "T" that releases at the right
  moment while you hold the trigger (**Ctrl+M** switches between them), an
  always-shown rocket pipper, and LOW, FRAG and OVERFLY warnings. A bomb
  released before its fuze arms lands a dud.
- **Laser and GPS bombs.** **Ctrl+L** turns the laser on by hand; a laser
  weapon turns it on by itself. Laser bombs follow your spot or a wingman's
  (**buddy lasing**), and JDAMs fly to the point designated at release. The HUD
  shows a guided weapon's reach, and a TTI countdown while it flies. New
  GBU-12, GBU-16 and GBU-38 stores join the GBU-10 family, GBU-27, GBU-28 and
  the larger JDAMs.
- **Missiles.** Mavericks fly on a designated lock or at the hottest object
  near the bore, HARMs and Shrikes fire from a HUD cue that shows every radar
  their seeker hears, and anti-ship missiles ride the radar's track. Terrain
  can block a lock or a laser, and the HUD says MASK when it does.
- **Watch the weapon.** A launch opens the target camera on the seeker's view
  of a Maverick, laser weapon or AT-2, all the way to impact. **F12** follows
  any bomb, rocket or missile from outside, stops about 328 ft short of the
  impact, and holds on the explosion for 1.5 seconds before returning.
- **Tab fires the gun** whatever weapon is selected, for strafing between bombs.
- The Pave Spike laser pod loads on pod stations. Load Ordnance warns when you
  carry laser bombs with nothing to lase them, and says what sensors the
  aircraft brings. Rocket pods hold their real rocket count, and 14 bomb,
  cluster and rocket stores are newly loadable.
- **AI wingmen strike too.** Give a Quick Mission wing the Strike role, or
  order your wingmen with **Alt+W** (every target of your target's kind),
  **Alt+F** and **Alt+G** ("Lase my target"). They pick stores that can hit the
  target, dive, pop up, lay down drag bombs, strafe, lase for each other and
  come home. See the [air-to-ground specification](../spec/air-to-ground.md).

### The surface world

- **Ground targets and air defenses in all 16 theaters.** The Quick Mission
  creator's ground target, AAA and SAM rows are live, with each theater's
  original targets: airfields, bridges, factories, columns, convoys, ships and
  more. Destroying the targets is the mission's objective, and the debrief
  counts them. With no ground target chosen, the AAA and SAM settings scatter
  enemy defenses over the enemy's side of the front.
- **The defenses fight.** SAM batteries work around their search radar (kill
  the radar or HARM it and they go blind), guns fire tuned bursts, heavy flak
  bursts around you with nearby guns working as a team, airfield infantry fire
  tracers, and supply trucks rearm the sites. Their radars show on your RWR,
  and if one shoots you down the debrief says which.
- **A living battlefield.** Columns drive and ships sail their routes, units
  turn their turrets toward what they fight, a front line of Blue and Red ground
  units battles a few miles away, and parked aircraft on airfields and carrier
  decks can be destroyed.
- **Nothing vanishes.** Destroyed buildings leave their damaged model or a ruin
  and burn for the whole mission, vehicles leave wrecks, parked aircraft leave
  burnt hulks, and bombed runways keep their craters.
- **Splash damage for every weapon** whose record has a blast radius: bombs,
  rockets, missiles, SAMs and flak hurt everything nearby, including your own
  side when friendly fire is on.
- **Carriers** appear with their hulls, islands, deck crew and parked aircraft,
  as targets for now. See the
  [surface defenses specification](../spec/surface-defenses.md).

### The AC-130 gunship and its sensor console

- **Three linked guns at their real rates.** The 25 mm fires 1,800 rounds a
  minute, the 40 mm 100 and the 105 mm 10, each with its own ammunition, muzzle
  flash and tracer. **Ctrl+7** and **Ctrl+8** pick and link guns, and the
  105 mm splashes soft targets.
- **The target camera is a gunsight.** Slew it, zoom, pin the ground or hold
  any object, and the linked guns follow the pipper within their arcs.
- **Ctrl+9 opens the full-screen sensor console** and hands the aircraft to an
  autopilot that flies the gun orbit around your point while you shoot. **P**
  pins or releases the orbit, the arrows or the mouse slew, and **Shift+P**
  locks the guns on a target while the sensor looks elsewhere.
  **Ctrl+Backslash** steps through white hot, black hot, TV and night vision,
  and a ground radar map (**Ctrl+0** for 5, 10 or 25 nm) feeds **T**. The
  console has a full gamepad layout.
- The console is a **T.O.R.E addition** in the spirit of the modern gunship;
  the original had nothing like it. See the
  [console guide](../spec/target-window.md#sensor-console).

### Helicopters, the V-22 and jump jets

- **Fly the AH-64 Apache, Mi-24 Hind and CH-47 Chinook** on a rotor model of
  their own: collective, cyclic and pedals, torque, translational lift, ground
  effect, autorotation and vortex ring state. The throttle keys drive the
  collective, and the HUD shows rotor speed, torque and radar height, with
  rotor warnings and rotor sound.
- **Convert the V-22 Osprey** between helicopter and airplane flight
  (**Ctrl+Page Up** and **Ctrl+Page Down** tilt the nacelles). The **AV-8B
  Harrier II** and **Yak-141** hover on vectored thrust within their weight
  limits, with the original's nozzle keys.
- **Ctrl+Alt+A** holds a hover over a point, **Ctrl+Shift+A** steps the
  stability help (Off, Damper, Attitude), and the **Easy flight physics** cheat
  makes them gentler still.
- Land on open ground or a flat roof when you are slow and level enough;
  anything harder is a crash.
- The AI flies all six as wingmen and enemies, from the runway or vertically,
  and AI helicopters stay low around the SAM rings they know about.
- Player-flown helicopters are a **T.O.R.E addition**: the original never let
  you fly one, so the handling is fitted, not matched. See
  [powered-lift flight](../spec/powered-lift-flight.md).

## Improvements

### 23 more aircraft

- **The roster grows from 13 to 36 retail aircraft**, plus the F/A-XX concept:
  C-130 Hercules, AC-130U Spectre, E-3 Sentry, IL-76 Mainstay, E-2C Hawkeye,
  AV-8B Harrier II, Yak-141, V-22 Osprey, AH-64 Apache, Mi-24 Hind,
  CH-47 Chinook, MiG-17F, four Phantoms (F-4B, F-4J, F-4E and F-4G),
  A-7E Corsair II, F-15C Eagle, F-16C Falcon, F-104N Starfighter,
  A-10 Thunderbolt, Boeing 747 and Airbus A310. You can fly every one, and so
  can the AI. See the [aircraft roster](../spec/aircraft-variety.md).
- The E-3, IL-76 and E-2 radar domes turn while the engines run.
- Clearly wrong figures in the original data are corrected: the Su-25 no
  longer carries a copy of the Su-24's weight, fuel and thrust, and the C-130,
  MiG-17F, V-22 and CH-47 have realistic thrust, weights and fuel.

### Stores on real stations

- **Weapons and tanks hang on pylons and rails at their real size**, and every
  store leaves from exactly where it is drawn. Pairs alternate left and right.
- Many aircraft carry their stores where the real one does: the F-14's
  Phoenixes in the tunnel and tanks under the intakes, the Flankers' tunnel and
  intake pylons, twin-rail launchers on the F/A-18 and others, and the A-4E's
  three tanks.
- **New optional stations:** F-15C wing and centreline tanks, wing missiles and
  a centreline bomb rack, F-16C inner wing tanks and wingtip AIM-120s, F-22
  wing pylons (now on the F-22N too), MiG-29 and Rafale C centreline tanks, and
  the F-4B's SUU-16 gun pod. Default loads are unchanged.
- **Jettison** your tanks with **Shift+K**, or the selected store with
  **Ctrl+Shift+K**. They fall inert and take their weight and fuel with them.
- Stores carried outside make you easier to find on radar; internal bays do
  not.
- Load Ordnance lists stations by their place on the aircraft (gun,
  centreline, then inboard to outboard) without station numbers.

### Sky, lighting and the cockpit at night

- **Dawn and dusk change smoothly**, with a moving moon and a slowly turning
  starfield. Stars come out brightest first as the sky darkens, and the land
  takes the warm light of a low sun.
- **Fires and explosions glow at night**, light the ground around them and
  stay visible to the edge of the night scene. Night tracers are red or green
  streaks with a glow, seen much farther away.
- **Cockpit lighting.** The cockpit art darkens with daylight and weather while
  the displays stay lit. **Ctrl+Shift+F** then minus or plus sets the red flood
  lights, **Ctrl+I** the instrument brightness, **Shift+[** and **Shift+]** the
  HUD brightness, and **Ctrl+H** steps the HUD through green, amber, white and
  cyan, which helps over fog and cloud. All of them are saved.
- The sun's glare and lens flare now hide behind terrain, objects and
  aircraft.
- **Every ground object casts a shadow**: airports, buildings, bridges, sites,
  vehicles and, at High and Ultra, trees. **Pref > Graphics...** has new
  **Shadows** and **Shadow distance** rows.

### The world

- **Redrawn airports.** Every airport is drawn at real scale from the
  original runway art, with real-width runways, curved taxiways, and runway,
  approach, PAPI and taxiway lights. Paired airport tiles become one field
  with a long runway.
- **Airports belong to a side.** The tower, the NAV list and the ground start
  picker offer only your side's and neutral fields. The 22 short strips are
  offered as ground starts, labelled STOL.
- **City fill.** Cities fill with real-size buildings, up to 500,000 per
  theater, fading in with distance. Big blasts knock them flat and they burn.
  They are scenery, never targets.
- **Vegetation.** Trees, bushes, palms and rocks drawn with original T.O.R.E
  artwork, grass blades out to 500 ft, finer ground detail up close, and
  smoother blending between terrain tiles. **Pref > Graphics...** sets their
  distance, detail and density.
- **Level ground under every site.** Buildings, sites and objectives sit on
  flat ground pads instead of tilting with the slope, and every object,
  unit and soldier stands on the ground rather than floating or sinking.
- Buildings and surface units are drawn and hit at their real size.

### Sound and blasts

- **Other aircraft's guns, launches and bomb releases are now heard**, as are
  SAM launches, from where they fire, arriving at the speed of sound as
  explosions already did. In the cockpit the canopy muffles them.
- **Explosions are sized by weapon**: a gun hit is small and a 2,000 lb bomb is
  big, with a flash that lights the night, sparks, smoke and a crater to
  match. Gun hits leave pock marks, and craters stay for the whole mission.
- **Shockwaves.** Large blasts throw a ring of dust across the ground, a brief
  white cloud in humid theaters, tan dust that hangs for up to a minute in dry
  ones, and spray over water. This is a T.O.R.E addition.
- Flak bursts sound like the original's air flak. See
  [explosions](../spec/explosions.md) and [sound](../spec/sound.md).

### Replays

- **Feedback flags** on enabled dedicated servers keep the player’s report in
  both recordings, with timeline markers, a seekable list and JSON-lines export.

- **The HUD camera (V)** shows the selected aircraft's HUD with an old
  videotape look, and the weapon symbology recorded 24 times a second.
- **The Sensor View (Shift+V)** shows the targeting sight or AC-130 console of
  any aircraft that has one, in each picture mode.
- **F12 follows weapons in replays** as it does in flight, with the same stop
  and hold at impact. **B** draws laser spots, designations and where each
  bomb landed.
- A banner shows the selected aircraft's, weapon's or ground unit's speed,
  altitude, heading, what it is doing and its target, and the object camera
  can follow ground units.
- Replays show the ground battle, the stores on every aircraft, knocked-down
  city blocks and the smoke of burning cities. See
  [mission replays](../REPLAYS.md).

### Quick Mission and menus

- **A loading screen** with a photograph and a progress bar while the flight
  is prepared, and the window stays responsive.
- **Map** on the creator opens a planning map with both sides' starts and the
  rough objective area. The in-flight map (**Shift+M**) gains the same mission
  markings, a zoom rocker, scroll bars and airfields coloured by side.
- **Mission brief** on the in-flight map: switch to **Mission** with Enter or
  controller A to read your Quick Mission setup and multiplayer rules. The
  in-flight spawn/respawn loadout and aircraft pages also have **Mission...**.
  Long briefs scroll; closing returns to your unchanged map or loadout.
- **Enemy start** and **Enemy airport** can park the enemy's first wing on its
  own field. Separation adds 75 nm.
- The creator starts on random aircraft the first time, then remembers your
  draft through debriefs and restarts, and a custom loadout comes back when
  you fly the same mission again.
- AI wingmen carry their flight lead's accepted loadout.
- **Pref > Graphics...** applies each change as you make it, and adds a
  **Frame rate** row (60, 100, 200 or V-sync, the default) and an optional
  frame rate counter.

### Flight, HUD and weapons

- **Stall speeds are fitted per aircraft**, so approach and liftoff speeds sit
  close to the real aircraft's. The F/A-18D lifts off at about 129 knots with
  its default load.
- **Overspeed is timed.** Above your top speed you get 5 safe seconds, then a
  growing chance each second of losing the airframe, and certain loss at 10
  seconds.
- **The gear stays down while it carries weight**, as in the original, with
  "Ground sensor preventing gear retraction". This replaces the belly slide of
  [v0.1.3](v0.1.3.md).
- Heavy aircraft keep their pull near top speed and can recover from a fast
  descent, and their top speeds are corrected.
- **Missiles.** Active-radar missiles (AIM-120, MICA, AA-12) call Fox three,
  the R-27 variants carry their real names, all-aspect IR missiles lock head
  on at a reduced range, and range and lock cues are advice: you can fire
  without them, and an active missile then goes maddog.
- The HUD pitch ladder is a true degree scale, and cockpit mirrors are live.
- Two-seaters eject both crew.
- **Kill credit** counts a missile that was decoyed but struck anyway, and up
  to 5,000 rounds, missiles and bombs can fly at once.
- A missile or bomb that bursts in the air beside its own launcher no longer
  hurts it; a ground burst still can.

### AI

- CAP wings patrol their stations and engage intruders.
- AI pilots look farther ahead for terrain the faster they fly, drop only
  their tanks when a fight starts, and start with fuel in their tanks.
- AI helicopters take off and land vertically and leave low after their run.

### Performance

- CPU work spreads across worker threads when the game falls behind, and many
  heavy paths are faster: big fights, hosting 30 players, cockpit drawing,
  shadows and vegetation. City layouts are kept on disk, so a theater with
  city fill loads in under a second and a half instead of up to 11 seconds.
- Menus and hidden windows do less work. A minimised single-player flight
  pauses; a multiplayer one keeps running.

## Fixes

### World and graphics

- Iraq, the Persian Gulf and Spain draw their original sand colours again
  instead of green, and North Vietnam no longer borrows another theater's
  colours.
- The MiG-21's underside no longer shows through its wings, and runway paint
  no longer flickers.
- Craters and ground marks drape onto slopes and no longer draw over vehicles.
- The suspension bridge and crane shadows are dark shadows, not magenta
  patches.
- Replay flight trails no longer jitter far from the map's centre.

### Flight

- The rudder turns the flight path again, even with the engines off, and
  nosewheel steering fades out by 50 mph instead of 25.
- The F-14's wing roots, exhausts and hook are aligned, and its body seams are
  closed.

### Combat

- Guns and missiles can hit a falling wreck without scoring another kill, and
  missiles no longer circle back to a missed intercept.
- A drop-launched missile such as the AIM-54 no longer explodes on its own
  launcher.
- Mavericks hit vehicles again: thin antenna lines had inflated their hit
  boxes.
- A destroyed aircraft makes no more radio calls, and your crew falls silent
  once your aircraft is lost.
- One splash that hits a formation gives one hit call, not one per aircraft.

### AI

- AI aircraft hold the runway in a crosswind takeoff and taxi on the centre
  lines.
- A-7 wingmen ordered onto a ground target attack instead of circling.
- AI pilots fly a damaged airframe within what it can still do.

### Controls

- A controller profile with the same number of bindings as the last one is no
  longer mistaken for it.

## Known limitations

- **Campaigns, standalone missions and carrier operations are still ahead.**
  Multiplayer is Quick Missions only, and carriers are targets, not decks to
  land on.
- **Multiplayer's last three-platform check was early in development.** A
  Linux, Windows and macOS game on John's network worked on 2026-10-05; most of
  what came after, host migration included, has been tested on one Linux
  machine and by scripted pilots. Please report what you find.
- **The dedicated server is unsigned and has no 32-bit Windows or ARM Linux
  download.** On a Mac, clear the download's quarantine before the first run
  ([server guide](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md#downloading-it)). On 32-bit Windows
  or ARM Linux, build it from source, or host from the game.
- **Helicopter and VTOL handling is fitted** and needs more pilot testing and
  tuning. On a Mac, macOS takes Ctrl with the arrow keys, which these aircraft
  use for trim and nozzles: turn those shortcuts off in System Settings or
  rebind the keys on the controls screen.
- **The live Watch view** does not yet show smoke, contrails, gun rounds,
  radio or HUD lines, and replays of network flights lack other aircraft's
  fuel, G and controls.
- **Older recordings have no recorded HUD**, so the HUD camera shows a simpler
  picture for them.
- **Some stores stay hidden** from Load Ordnance until they can fly: the ADEN,
  BK27, GAU-13 and GSh-6-23 gun pods and the AA-6, AA-9, PL-7 and PL-10
  missiles.
- **City fill and vegetation cost frame time** at 4K over dense cities and
  forests. Lower Building distance, Vegetation distance or Grass in
  **Pref > Graphics...** if your frame rate drops.
- **The MiG-17F cannot climb away with its default drop tanks.** The
  original's data has the same problem.
- **Much of the new sound has not been checked by ear**, and retail comparison
  is unavailable. Fitted and opinionated behaviour is listed in the
  [feature matrix](../features.md), and nothing here claims exact parity with
  the original.
- Everything under [v0.1.3 known limitations](v0.1.3.md#known-limitations)
  that is not fixed above still applies.

## Feedback

Please report bugs and differences from the original in an
[issue](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/issues) or a
[discussion](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/discussions). A
mission recording from the Replays screen makes a bug much easier to trace.
For a multiplayer problem, say who hosted and how each player connected.
