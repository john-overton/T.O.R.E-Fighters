# v0.2.1: Patch Up, Buttercup

**Released:** TBD · **Channel:** Pre-release · **Tag:** [v0.2.1](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.1)

Version 0.2.1 tidies up multiplayer's first release: your kills now survive
a revival and an early exit, T, Enter and Apostrophe pick targets closer to
the way the manual describes, and games match by a short compatibility code
instead of the exact build. The Mac download opens without Terminal, and the
main menu tells you when a newer release is out. Try it first by joining a
game, scoring, dying, coming back and leaving early: your report keeps every
kill.

> **Everyone in a multiplayer game needs 0.2.1, or a build with the same
> protocol and compatibility code.** 0.2.1 cannot play with 0.2.0, and the
> Internet Lounge of one does not list the other's games. The details are under
> [Getting started](#getting-started).

## Getting started

1. Download the package for your platform from the
   [release page](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.1)
   and install it over 0.2.0. Your imported media is kept; nothing is
   re-imported.
2. **On a Mac, open the app the normal way.** Copy it from the disk image to
   Applications and open it. If macOS blocks it because it cannot check the
   developer, go to **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, choose
   **Open Anyway**, then **Open**. The 0.2.0 Terminal workaround for an app
   that "is damaged" is no longer needed; the
   [first-launch help](../DEVELOPMENT.md#unsigned-builds-and-first-launch)
   keeps it as a last resort.
3. **Multiplayer uses network protocol 73.** Games now match by protocol and an
   eight-digit **compatibility code** worked out from your imported Fighters
   Anthology content, shown as "Code" in the lounges' game details and the
   lobby. Matching builds play together even when their versions differ; a
   game with another code or protocol is hidden unless you tick
   **Show other versions**, and a refused join names both codes.
4. **Recordings still use replay format 4.** This build plays every older
   recording, including those from 0.2.0.
5. **Server operators: upgrade the master server first.** The 0.2.1
   `tore-master` keeps listing 0.2.0 games for 0.2.0 players, in a separate
   list, so 0.2.0 dedicated servers can keep running while you upgrade. Then
   upgrade each dedicated server to 0.2.1 to host 0.2.1 players.
   `tore-server --version` now prints the server's compatibility code. See
   [running a master for old and new games](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md#running-a-master-for-old-and-new-games).

## Highlights

### Your score survives revivals and early exits

- **Kills, hits and losses follow you across lives.** The debrief and the
  **K** score board count every life you flew in the mission, not only the
  aircraft you were in at the end.
- **Leave early and still get a report.** Leaving, ending your flight,
  **Return to Main Menu** and **Exit** now open a **PARTIAL REPORT** from what
  your game saw: "You left at 12:34; the mission continued". A lost connection
  gets one too, headed "Connection lost". The report lists each life, its
  ending, revivals, kills, weapons, hits and the mission messages you
  received. Exit closes the game once you dismiss it.
- **AI pilots and players who left stay in the scores.** SCORES, RESULTS and
  the **K** board list AI aircraft, keep departed players marked as left, and
  show cumulative hits. On compact pages **A** marks AI and **L** marks a
  player who left. **Ctrl+Page Up** and **Ctrl+Page Down** page through the
  **K** board, 12 rows at a time.
- **A revived aircraft keeps its place in the flight.** Red 2 comes back as
  Red 2, for players and AI alike, instead of a new number such as Red 6.

### Target selection the manual's way

- **T and Shift+T cycle your own radar's displayed contacts, friendlies
  included**, nearest first. The radar must be in track-while-scan, and only
  contacts inside the selected range scale count. Contacts you only know
  about from the data link are skipped.
- **Enter cycles the aircraft you can see on screen, left to right**, and
  **Apostrophe** picks the one nearest the centre of the forward view. Both
  include friendlies and work with the radar off. A visual pick stays while
  you can still see the aircraft; it gives no radar lock.
- Apostrophe is a new action of its own, rebindable on the controls screen.
  Chat still opens with the key above Tab, and Enter still sends an open chat
  line. AI pilots, ground target pages and the AC-130 console keep their
  existing rules.

### Matching by compatibility code

- **The exact build no longer has to match.** Two games with the same
  protocol and the same compatibility code can play together, whatever their
  version or commit. A game with a different code is shown dimmed under
  **Show other versions** and cannot be joined, and the refusal reads like
  "The server's compatibility code is 1234ABCD; yours is 3FA9C21B."
- The code covers your whole imported game content, so it does not change
  when the King changes the mission. The detailed per-mission content check
  still runs once a mission loads.

## Improvements

### Respawn menus

- **Searchable, scrolling lists while you wait to fly again.** On the
  in-flight **Loadout** page, choosing a station opens its stores grouped and
  ordered as on Load Ordnance. **Aircraft** for Player Choice slots is one
  complete list in the creator's order. Both have a red scroll bar and a
  search box that matches names and designations: try `120`, `16` or
  `hornet`.
- **Tab** switches between the search box and the list. Arrows, **Page Up**,
  **Page Down**, **Home**, **End**, the mouse wheel and the controller D-pad
  move through it. **Escape** clears the search first, then closes the station
  list, then cancels the page.

### Lounges and lobbies

- **Choose your master server.** **Options** in the Internet Lounge has a
  **Master server** field that accepts a host name or address with an optional
  port, and **Default** restores `master.jroverton.com:26901`. The choice is
  saved and used for listing, hosting with **New**, connecting through routers,
  the relay and anonymous statistics. A typing mistake is refused and the last
  good address kept. The [master server guide](../MASTER-SERVER.md) covers
  running your own.
- **Return slots are confirmed with the server.** When you go back to a game
  you dropped out of, the lounge asks the server before promising anything,
  then says where you will fly and for how long, for example "You will be
  added back to Blue Wing 1 #1 (up to 0:01:28 more, unless lost, released or
  the mission ends)". If nothing is held it says "No reserved slot is
  available for your return."
- **The Leave confirmation says what will really happen.** Ordinary players
  and observers see "Are you sure?". A hosting player is told whether another
  player will take over hosting or the game ends for everyone, and a King who
  is not the host is told who gets the crown. The same confirmation covers
  **Leave**, **Return to Main Menu**, **Exit** and closing the window.

### Main menu

- **Update Available** appears at the right of the Choose Activity menu bar
  when a newer release is out, prereleases included, and opens its release
  page. Download and install it as usual; nothing installs by itself.
- **Leave Feedback**, beside it, opens the project's issues page.
- The check runs in the background at most once a day. It sends one
  anonymous request to GitHub with no player name or profile data, and
  **Pref > Check for updates: Off** stops it.

### Dedicated servers

- **Recording no longer slows the mission.** A dedicated server writes its
  replay on a separate thread and can hold up to a minute of flight while the
  disk catches up, so a slow disk no longer stalls flight or cuts recordings
  and feedback short. In a test under heavy disk load, a 24-aircraft mission
  kept its worst tick under 20 ms and saved a complete recording.
- The status line ends with the **worst** tick time since the last status, so
  a single pause shows even when the average looks fine.
- The macOS server download carries an ad hoc signature that is checked
  before it is published, as the app does.

### For developers

- The continuous integration workflows run on demand and on release tags
  instead of on every push; the pre-push hook still checks every push
  locally.
- The lounge tests no longer depend on the installed content or on free game
  ports, so they pass on a machine with a game import or a running server.

## Fixes

- **The macOS app no longer reports "is damaged" on first launch.** The 0.2.0
  app had no complete bundle seal; 0.2.1 seals it and the release build checks
  the seal before publishing.
- **A new callsign is used after you leave and come back.** Leaving cleanly
  used to keep your old name for the next join to that server. A clean leave
  now forgets it, while a dropped connection still keeps your aircraft and
  identity for rejoining, under your current callsign.
- **The debrief no longer loses earlier lives.** A player who scored two kills
  in two lives could see only one in the debrief.
- **Leaving no longer warns that it ends the game when it does not.** The
  King saw "Leaving ends the game for everyone." even on a dedicated server,
  where the game carries on without them.
- **T no longer skips friendlies or picks contacts the radar is not showing**,
  and Enter and Apostrophe no longer need a radar or infrared contact.

## Known limitations

- **0.2.1 and 0.2.0 cannot play together.** Their protocols differ, and each
  Internet Lounge lists only its own generation, even with
  **Show other versions**.
- **The Mac build is still not signed by a registered developer.** macOS
  asks you to confirm it once through **Open Anyway**.
- **This release's multiplayer changes were tested on Linux** with scripted
  pilots and test servers. Windows and macOS have not been rerun.
- **A partial report only knows what your game received.** After a lost
  connection it names the time of its latest statistics.
- **Visual target selection ignores weather and night.** Enter and
  Apostrophe use the aircraft's normal visual range; the manual's weather and
  night range factors are not built yet.
- **Update Available only opens the release page.** Automatic download and
  install are not built, and the link has been checked on Linux only.
- **A dedicated server's recording buffer costs memory.** A full minute held
  for a busy 24-aircraft mission is about 150 MB, and a server crash can lose
  up to that minute of recording.
- Everything under [v0.2.0 known limitations](v0.2.0.md#known-limitations)
  that is not fixed above still applies.
