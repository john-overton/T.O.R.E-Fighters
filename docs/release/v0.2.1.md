# v0.2.1: Multiplayer, Patch Up, Buttercup

**Released:** TBD · **Channel:** Pre-release · **Tag:** [v0.2.1](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.1)

Version 0.2.1 is **Milestone 2: multiplayer**, the 0.2.0 release, together
with its first patch. Build a Quick Mission, fly it with friends on your
network or the internet, against the AI or against each other, and come back
to the lobby for another round; single player gains defended ground targets,
air-to-ground weapons, the AC-130, helicopters, 23 more aircraft and a fuller
world. The patch keeps your kills across revivals and early exits, picks
targets the manual's way, matches games by a short compatibility code and lets
the Mac app open without Terminal. Coming from 0.1.3, read on from
[What 0.2.0 brought](#what-020-brought); coming from 0.2.0, start with
[What's new in 0.2.1](#whats-new-in-021).

> **Everyone in a multiplayer game needs 0.2.1, or a build with the same
> protocol and compatibility code.** 0.2.1 cannot play with 0.2.0, and the
> Internet Lounge of one does not list the other's games. The details are
> under [Getting started](#getting-started).

## Getting started

1. Download the package for your platform from the
   [release page](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.1)
   and install it over the previous version.
2. **Coming from 0.1.3, your media is imported again, once.** This release
   keeps more of your copy: the Quick Mission ground target data and the
   multiplayer screens' art. The game re-imports on its own from the copy it
   remembers; if it cannot find it, point it at your Fighters Anthology again.
   **Pref > Re-import media** works at any time. Coming from 0.2.0, your
   import is kept as it is.
3. **On a Mac, open the app the normal way.** Copy it from the disk image to
   Applications and open it. If macOS blocks it because it cannot check the
   developer, go to **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, choose
   **Open Anyway**, then **Open**. The 0.2.0 Terminal workaround for an app
   that "is damaged" is no longer needed; the
   [first-launch help](../DEVELOPMENT.md#unsigned-builds-and-first-launch)
   keeps it as a last resort.
4. **Multiplayer uses network protocol 73.** On the **Multi** menu,
   **Direct Connection...** opens the **Direct Network Lounge**, which finds
   games on your local network and joins a typed address, and **Internet...**
   opens the **Internet Lounge**, which lists public games. **New** in either
   one hosts a game. Games match by protocol and an eight-digit
   **compatibility code** worked out from your imported Fighters Anthology
   content, shown as "Code" in the lounges' game details and the lobby.
   Players who imported the **Fighters Anthology 1.0 disc** and those who
   imported the **1.02F patch** get the same code and play together. A game
   with another code or protocol is hidden unless you tick
   **Show other versions**, and a refused join names both codes. The
   [multiplayer guide](../MULTIPLAYER.md) covers the rest.
5. **Recordings use replay format 4.** This build plays every older
   recording. Builds limited to formats 2 or 3 cannot open format 4.
6. **Dedicated servers** have their own downloads on this release:
   `tore-server-0.2.1-windows-x86_64.zip`, `-macos-arm64.tar.gz`,
   `-macos-x86_64.tar.gz` and `-linux-x86_64.tar.gz`, each with a sample
   configuration and mission. The game is not needed on the server machine,
   only your own Fighters Anthology to import. Follow the
   [server guide](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md#downloading-it). You do not need one
   to play: any player can host from the game. `tore-server --version` prints
   the server's compatibility code.
7. **Server operators: upgrade the master server first.** The 0.2.1
   `tore-master` keeps listing 0.2.0 games for 0.2.0 players, in a separate
   list, so 0.2.0 dedicated servers can keep running while you upgrade. Then
   upgrade each dedicated server to 0.2.1 to host 0.2.1 players. See
   [running a master for old and new games](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md#running-a-master-for-old-and-new-games).

## What's new in 0.2.1

### Your score survives revivals and early exits

- **Kills, hits and losses follow you across lives.** The debrief and the
  **K** score board count every life you flew in the mission, not only the
  aircraft you were in at the end.
- **Leave early and still get a report.** Leaving, ending your flight,
  **Return to Main Menu** and **Exit** now open a **PARTIAL REPORT** from what
  your game saw: "You left at 12:34; the mission continued". A lost connection
  gets one too, headed "Connection lost". The report lists each life, its
  ending, revivals, kills, weapons, hits and the mission messages you
  received. Exit closes the game once you dismiss it.
- **AI pilots and players who left stay in the scores.** SCORES, RESULTS and
  the **K** board list AI aircraft, keep departed players marked as left, and
  show cumulative hits. On compact pages **A** marks AI and **L** marks a
  player who left. **Ctrl+Page Up** and **Ctrl+Page Down** page through the
  **K** board, 12 rows at a time.
- **A revived aircraft keeps its place in the flight.** Red 2 comes back as
  Red 2, for players and AI alike, instead of a new number such as Red 6.

### Target selection the manual's way

- **T and Shift+T cycle your own radar's displayed contacts, friendlies
  included**, nearest first. The radar must be in track-while-scan, and only
  contacts inside the selected range scale count. Contacts you only know
  about from the data link are skipped.
- **Enter cycles the aircraft you can see on screen, left to right**, and
  **Apostrophe** picks the one nearest the centre of the forward view. Both
  include friendlies and work with the radar off. A visual pick stays while
  you can still see the aircraft; it gives no radar lock.
- Apostrophe is a new action of its own, rebindable on the controls screen.
  Chat still opens with the key above Tab, and Enter still sends an open chat
  line. AI pilots, ground target pages and the AC-130 console keep their
  existing rules.

### Matching by compatibility code

- **The exact build no longer has to match.** Two games with the same
  protocol and the same compatibility code can play together, whatever their
  version or commit. A game with a different code is shown dimmed under
  **Show other versions** and cannot be joined, and the refusal reads like
  "The server's compatibility code is 1234ABCD; yours is 3FA9C21B."
- The code covers your whole imported game content, so it does not change
  when the King changes the mission. A 1.0 import and a 1.02F import give the
  same code. The detailed per-mission content check still runs once a mission
  loads.

### Respawn menus

- **Searchable, scrolling lists while you wait to fly again.** On the
  in-flight **Loadout** page, choosing a station opens its stores grouped and
  ordered as on Load Ordnance. **Aircraft** for Player Choice slots is one
  complete list in the creator's order. Both have a red scroll bar and a
  search box that matches names and designations: try `120`, `16` or
  `hornet`.
- **Tab** switches between the search box and the list. Arrows, **Page Up**,
  **Page Down**, **Home**, **End**, the mouse wheel and the controller D-pad
  move through it. **Escape** clears the search first, then closes the station
  list, then cancels the page.

### Lounges and lobbies

- **Choose your master server.** **Options** in the Internet Lounge has a
  **Master server** field that accepts a host name or address with an optional
  port, and **Default** restores `master.jroverton.com:26901`. The choice is
  saved and used for listing, hosting with **New**, connecting through routers,
  the relay and anonymous statistics. A typing mistake is refused and the last
  good address kept. The [master server guide](../MASTER-SERVER.md) covers
  running your own.
- **Return slots are confirmed with the server.** When you go back to a game
  you dropped out of, the lounge asks the server before promising anything,
  then says where you will fly and for how long, for example "You will be
  added back to Blue Wing 1 #1 (up to 0:01:28 more, unless lost, released or
  the mission ends)". If nothing is held it says "No reserved slot is
  available for your return."
- **The Leave confirmation says what will really happen.** Players and
  observers who are not hosting see "Are you sure?". A hosting player sees
  "Another player will take over hosting." when someone is ready to take
  over, "Leaving ends the game for everyone." when nobody is, and "Leaving
  ends the game." when alone. A King who is not the host, with others in the
  game, sees "The crown passes to" and the next King's callsign. The same
  panel covers **Leave**, **Return to Main Menu**, **Exit** and closing the
  window.

### Main menu

- **Update Available** appears at the right of the Choose Activity menu bar
  when a newer release is out, prereleases included, and opens its release
  page. Download and install it as usual; nothing installs by itself.
- **Leave Feedback**, beside it, opens the project's issues page.
- The check runs in the background at most once a day. It sends one
  anonymous request to GitHub with no player name or profile data, and
  **Pref > Check for updates: Off** stops it.

### Dedicated servers

- **Recording no longer slows the mission.** A dedicated server writes its
  replay on a separate thread and can hold up to a minute of flight while the
  disk catches up, so a slow disk no longer stalls flight or cuts recordings
  and feedback short. In a test under heavy disk load, a 24-aircraft mission
  kept its worst tick under 20 ms and saved a complete recording.
- The status line ends with the **worst** tick time since the last status, so
  a single pause shows even when the average looks fine.
- The macOS server download carries an ad hoc signature that is checked
  before it is published, as the app does.

### Fixes in 0.2.1

- **The macOS app no longer reports "is damaged" on first launch.** The 0.2.0
  app had no complete bundle seal; 0.2.1 seals it and the release build checks
  the seal before publishing.
- **A new callsign is used after you leave and come back.** Leaving cleanly
  used to keep your old name for the next join to that server. A clean leave
  now forgets it, while a dropped connection still keeps your aircraft and
  identity for rejoining, under your current callsign.
- **The debrief no longer loses earlier lives.** A player who scored two kills
  in two lives could see only one in the debrief.
- **Leaving no longer warns that it ends the game when it does not.** The
  King saw "Leaving ends the game for everyone." even on a dedicated server,
  where the game carries on without them.
- **T no longer skips friendlies or picks contacts the radar is not showing**,
  and Enter and Apostrophe no longer need a radar or infrared contact.

### For developers

- The continuous integration workflows run on demand and on release tags
  instead of on every push; the pre-push hook still checks every push
  locally.
- The lounge tests no longer depend on the installed content or on free game
  ports, so they pass on a machine with a game import or a running server.

## What 0.2.0 brought

The [v0.2.0 notes](v0.2.0.md) have every change and fix in full. In short:

### Multiplayer

- **Up to 30 human pilots in one Quick Mission**: 30 against each other in
  PvP, or 15 together against the AI in co-op. The original allowed 8. The AI
  flies every slot nobody takes, and takes over at once when a player drops.
- **Host from the game or join one.** **New** hosts the Quick Mission you have
  built. The game asks your router to forward its port (UPnP, NAT-PMP or PCP;
  a switch in **Options** turns this off), and the Internet Lounge connects
  players through routers, falling back to a relay on the list server when no
  direct path works.
- **The lobby.** Take a slot, arm your own aircraft with **Loadout**, press
  **Ready**, and the King (the hosting player) presses **Fly**. Everyone can
  read the mission on **Mission...**; only the King changes it.
  **Settings...** holds the King's rules on four pages (Game, Revival, Scoring
  and Realism), **Players...** passes the crown or kicks, and each player's
  system (Windows, macOS or Linux) shows beside their name. When some players
  are ready and others are not, Fly becomes **Force Start**, a 10 second
  countdown.
- **PvP sides.** Blue and red slots, **Bluefor** and **Redfor** boxes to pick a
  side, and a Sides rule: Free, Locked once flown, or Balanced, where
  **Autobalance** seats each player on the side with fewer humans.
- **Revival and respawn.** The original's revival rules (lives, delay,
  distance from the battle and weapons), with distances out to 150 nm. Lost AI
  aircraft can respawn too. **Player Choice** wings let each pilot pick their
  own aircraft, and while you wait to fly again **Loadout...** and
  **Aircraft...** stay on the Escape menu. Press **Enter** or **controller A**
  when ready to respawn; there is no automatic respawn.
- **Join a mission in flight** by taking over an AI aircraft, or press
  **Watch** to follow the fight live in the replay viewer and scrub back up to
  10 minutes. If you leave your game at a menu or in the background for 5
  minutes, the AI flies your aircraft until you choose **Take Back Flight**.
- **The game survives losing its host.** Another player's game takes over
  hosting and everyone flies on about 2 to 3.5 seconds later, with missiles in
  flight and scores intact. A player who drops can rejoin their kept aircraft.
- **Talk and fight as a team.** The **~** key opens chat to everyone, your
  side, your wing or your target, with the original's quick messages on F1 to
  F12. The flight data link shares contacts and locks: **Alt+A** sorts each
  wingman onto a different bandit, **Alt+N** listens to the other flights, and
  human wingmen answer orders with **Alt+Shift+E**, W, B and H.
- **Scores and time.** **K** opens the score board, a kill limit can end a PvP
  mission, and the debrief adds SCORES and RESULTS pages. Timed matches show
  the time remaining in the Escape menu and at the upper right during the
  last five minutes.
- **Everything else flies online too**: ground targets and their defenses,
  every air-to-ground weapon and sight, buddy lasing between players, the
  AC-130 console, and every aircraft's stores emptying as they are fired.
- **Dedicated servers** run with no window on Linux, Windows and macOS, rotate
  through missions in order or shuffled, can wait for a minimum number of ready
  players, and keep everyone connected between rounds. Recording and feedback
  flags are opt-in. See the [server guide](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md).
- Your multiplayer flights are saved as replays. Listing or joining a game
  through the Internet Lounge sends anonymous statistics, on by default and
  switched off in **Options**; the
  [README](../../README.md#online-play-and-anonymous-statistics) lists exactly
  what is sent.

### Air-to-ground weapons

- **Find the target.** **Ctrl+R** switches the radar to its air-to-ground page,
  and **T**, **Shift+T**, **Enter** or a click on the scope designates ground
  objects. Aircraft with a laser or an infrared sensor get a **targeting
  sight**: slew it with **Alt+arrows**, zoom, pin a point with
  **Shift+Backslash** or track a vehicle.
- **Bomb sights.** A CCIP pipper for unguided bombs, a level bombing "T"
  (**Ctrl+M** switches between them), an always-shown rocket pipper, and LOW,
  FRAG and OVERFLY warnings. A bomb released before its fuze arms lands a dud.
- **Laser and GPS bombs.** **Ctrl+L** turns the laser on by hand. Laser bombs
  follow your spot or a wingman's (**buddy lasing**), and JDAMs fly to the
  point designated at release, with the weapon's reach and a TTI countdown on
  the HUD. GBU-12, GBU-16 and GBU-38 join the guided stores.
- **Missiles.** Mavericks, HARMs and Shrikes with a HUD cue for every radar
  their seeker hears, and anti-ship missiles on the radar's track. Terrain can
  block a lock or a laser, and the HUD says MASK when it does.
- **Watch the weapon.** A launch opens the target camera on the seeker's view
  to impact, and **F12** follows any bomb, rocket or missile from outside.
  **Tab** fires the gun whatever weapon is selected.
- **AI wingmen strike too.** Give a wing the Strike role, or order wingmen
  with **Alt+W**, **Alt+F** and **Alt+G** ("Lase my target"). See the
  [air-to-ground specification](../spec/air-to-ground.md).

### The surface world

- **Ground targets and air defenses in all 16 theaters.** The Quick Mission
  creator's ground target, AAA and SAM rows are live, with each theater's
  original airfields, bridges, factories, columns, convoys and ships.
- **The defenses fight.** SAM batteries work around their search radar (kill
  the radar and they go blind), guns fire tuned bursts, heavy flak bursts
  around you, and supply trucks rearm the sites. Their radars show on your
  RWR.
- **A living battlefield.** Columns drive, ships sail, a front line of Blue
  and Red ground units battles a few miles away, and parked aircraft on
  airfields and carrier decks can be destroyed.
- **Nothing vanishes.** Ruins burn for the whole mission, vehicles leave
  wrecks, and bombed runways keep their craters. Bombs, rockets, missiles,
  SAMs and flak do **splash damage**. Carriers appear as targets for now. See
  the [surface defenses specification](../spec/surface-defenses.md).

### The AC-130 gunship and its sensor console

- **Three linked guns at their real rates**: the 25 mm at 1,800 rounds a
  minute, the 40 mm at 100 and the 105 mm at 10. **Ctrl+7** and **Ctrl+8**
  pick and link guns, and the target camera is their gunsight.
- **Ctrl+9 opens the full-screen sensor console** and hands the aircraft to an
  autopilot that flies the gun orbit while you shoot, with white hot, black
  hot, TV and night vision, a ground radar map and a full gamepad layout. The
  console is a **T.O.R.E addition**; see the
  [console guide](../spec/target-window.md#sensor-console).

### Helicopters, the V-22 and jump jets

- **Fly the AH-64 Apache, Mi-24 Hind and CH-47 Chinook** on a rotor model of
  their own, with collective, torque, ground effect, autorotation and vortex
  ring state.
- **Convert the V-22 Osprey** between helicopter and airplane flight
  (**Ctrl+Page Up** and **Ctrl+Page Down**), and hover the **AV-8B Harrier II**
  and **Yak-141** on vectored thrust. **Ctrl+Alt+A** holds a hover.
- Player-flown helicopters are a **T.O.R.E addition** with fitted handling.
  See [powered-lift flight](../spec/powered-lift-flight.md).

### Aircraft and stores

- **The roster grows from 13 to 36 retail aircraft**, plus the F/A-XX
  concept: the C-130, AC-130U, E-3, IL-76, E-2C, AV-8B, Yak-141, V-22, AH-64,
  Mi-24, CH-47, MiG-17F, four Phantoms, A-7E, F-15C, F-16C, F-104N, A-10,
  Boeing 747 and Airbus A310. You can fly every one, and so can the AI. See
  the [aircraft roster](../spec/aircraft-variety.md).
- **Weapons and tanks hang on pylons and rails at their real size** and leave
  from where they are drawn, with new optional stations on the F-15C, F-16C,
  F-22, MiG-29, Rafale C and F-4B. **Shift+K** jettisons tanks and
  **Ctrl+Shift+K** the selected store.

### Sky, lighting and the cockpit at night

- **Dawn and dusk change smoothly**, with a moving moon and a slowly turning
  starfield. Fires, explosions and tracers glow at night.
- **Cockpit lighting**: flood lights, instrument and HUD brightness, and HUD
  colours (**Ctrl+H**) that help over fog and cloud, all saved.
- **Every ground object casts a shadow**, with new **Shadows** and
  **Shadow distance** rows in **Pref > Graphics...**.

### The world

- **Redrawn airports** at real scale from the original runway art, with
  runway, approach, PAPI and taxiway lights. Airports belong to a side, and
  the 22 short strips are offered as STOL ground starts.
- **City fill and vegetation.** Cities fill with real-size buildings that
  big blasts knock flat; trees, bushes, palms, rocks and grass are drawn with
  original T.O.R.E artwork. **Pref > Graphics...** sets their distance and
  density.
- Buildings and sites stand on level ground pads, and every object stands on
  the ground.

### Sound and blasts

- **Other aircraft's guns, launches and bomb releases are heard**, arriving
  at the speed of sound, muffled by the canopy.
- **Explosions are sized by weapon**, with a flash, sparks, smoke and a
  crater to match, and large blasts throw a shockwave of dust or spray (a
  T.O.R.E addition). See [explosions](../spec/explosions.md).

### Replays

- **The HUD camera (V)** shows the selected aircraft's HUD with an old
  videotape look, and **the Sensor View (Shift+V)** shows any targeting sight
  or AC-130 console.
- **F12 follows weapons** as in flight, **B** draws laser spots and bomb
  impacts, and feedback flags from dedicated servers show on the timeline.
  See [mission replays](../REPLAYS.md).

### Quick Mission and menus

- **A loading screen** with a photograph and a progress bar while the flight
  is prepared.
- **Map** on the creator opens a planning map, and the in-flight map
  (**Shift+M**) gains mission markings and a **Mission** brief.
- **Enemy start** and **Enemy airport** can park the enemy's first wing on its
  own field. The creator remembers your draft and your custom loadout.
- **Pref > Graphics...** applies changes as you make them and adds a
  **Frame rate** row and an optional frame rate counter.

### Flight, HUD and weapons

- **Stall speeds are fitted per aircraft**, and **overspeed is timed**: 5
  safe seconds above top speed, then a growing chance of losing the airframe.
- **The gear stays down while it carries weight**, as in the original.
- **Missiles.** Active-radar missiles call Fox three, all-aspect IR missiles
  lock head on at reduced range, and range and lock cues are advice: an
  active missile fired without a lock goes maddog.
- The HUD pitch ladder is a true degree scale, cockpit mirrors are live, and
  two-seaters eject both crew.

### AI

- CAP wings patrol their stations and engage intruders. AI pilots look
  farther ahead for terrain the faster they fly and drop only their tanks
  when a fight starts.

### Performance

- CPU work spreads across worker threads when the game falls behind, big
  fights and hosting 30 players are faster, and a theater with city fill
  loads in under a second and a half instead of up to 11 seconds.

## Known limitations

- **0.2.1 and 0.2.0 cannot play together.** Their protocols differ, and each
  Internet Lounge lists only its own generation, even with
  **Show other versions**.
- **Campaigns, standalone missions and carrier operations are still ahead.**
  Multiplayer is Quick Missions only, and carriers are targets, not decks to
  land on.
- **Multiplayer's last three-platform check was early in development.** A
  Linux, Windows and macOS game on John's network worked on 2026-10-05; most
  of what came after, host migration and this patch included, has been tested
  on Linux with scripted pilots and test servers. Please report what you
  find.
- **Nothing is signed by a registered developer.** macOS asks you to confirm
  the app and the dedicated server once through **Open Anyway**, and Windows
  SmartScreen warns about the installer. The dedicated server has no 32-bit
  Windows or ARM Linux download: build it from source, or host from the game.
- **A partial report only knows what your game received.** After a lost
  connection it names the time of its latest statistics.
- **Visual target selection ignores weather and night.** Enter and
  Apostrophe use the aircraft's normal visual range; the manual's weather and
  night range factors are not built yet.
- **Update Available only opens the release page.** Automatic download and
  install are not built, and the link has been checked on Linux only.
- **A dedicated server's recording buffer costs memory.** A full minute held
  for a busy 24-aircraft mission is about 150 MB, and a server crash can lose
  up to that minute of recording.
- **Helicopter and VTOL handling is fitted** and needs more pilot testing. On
  a Mac, macOS takes Ctrl with the arrow keys, which these aircraft use for
  trim and nozzles: turn those shortcuts off in System Settings or rebind the
  keys.
- **The live Watch view** does not yet show smoke, contrails, gun rounds,
  radio or HUD lines, and replays of network flights lack other aircraft's
  fuel, G and controls. **Older recordings have no recorded HUD.**
- **Some stores stay hidden** from Load Ordnance until they can fly: the ADEN,
  BK27, GAU-13 and GSh-6-23 gun pods and the AA-6, AA-9, PL-7 and PL-10
  missiles.
- **City fill and vegetation cost frame time** at 4K over dense cities and
  forests. Lower Building distance, Vegetation distance or Grass in
  **Pref > Graphics...** if your frame rate drops.
- **The MiG-17F cannot climb away with its default drop tanks.** The
  original's data has the same problem.
- **Much of the new sound has not been checked by ear**, and retail comparison
  is unavailable. Fitted and opinionated behaviour is listed in the
  [feature matrix](../features.md), and nothing here claims exact parity with
  the original.
- Everything under [v0.1.3 known limitations](v0.1.3.md#known-limitations)
  that is not fixed in 0.2.0 or 0.2.1 still applies.
