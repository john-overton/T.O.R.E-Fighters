# v0.2.2: Notch, Chaff and Energy

**Released:** TBD · **Channel:** Pre-release · **Tag:** [v0.2.2](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.2)

Version 0.2.2 changes how every guided store flies. Missiles now carry real
energy: they climb, coast, slow down in thick air and bleed speed in every
turn, so a shot fired too far out, or dragged out by a running target, falls
short and goes dead. Flares and chaff are physical decoys the seeker chases,
the AI defends itself the same way you should, and tanks stop sniping
aircraft. Try a long AIM-120 or AA-12 shot against a target that turns cold,
then try one inside the range cue, and watch the difference.

> **Everyone in a multiplayer game needs 0.2.2.** 0.2.2 uses network
> protocol 77 and cannot play with 0.2.1. Dedicated servers must be updated
> too. The details are under [Getting started](#getting-started).

## Getting started

1. Download the package for your platform from the
   [release page](https://github.com/john-overton/T.O.R.E-Fighters/releases/tag/v0.2.2)
   and install it over the previous version. Your media import is kept as it
   is.
2. **Windows is 64-bit only.** There is no 32-bit Windows installer from this
   release on; every Windows player we know of is on 64-bit, and the 32-bit
   build ran slower.
3. **Multiplayer uses network protocol 77.** 0.2.2 and 0.2.1 games have
   different protocols and compatibility codes, so they cannot join each
   other. In the Internet Lounge, a game of the other version appears only
   under **Show other versions**, dimmed.
4. **Server operators: update every dedicated server to 0.2.2.** The master
   server needs no update for this release; a 0.2.1 `tore-master` lists 0.2.2
   games. The [server guide](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md#downloading-it) covers
   the downloads.
5. **Recordings stay in replay format 4**, and this build plays your older
   recordings.

## Highlights

### Guided stores fly on energy

- **One energy model for every guided A2A and A2G store.** Missiles inherit
  your speed at launch, burn their motors, fight drag that is higher in the
  thicker air down low, and lose speed in every turn. How hard a missile can
  turn depends on its speed and the air it is in, so a slow missile at high
  altitude cannot pull much.
- **Missiles lead their target** with proportional navigation, and
  long-range radar stores loft before diving onto the target.
- **Each class of store has a fixed guidance time.** A short-range IR store
  guides for 20 seconds, a medium-range air store for 70 and a long-range one
  for 180. After that it flies on unguided.
- **A missile that runs out of energy goes dead.** An air-to-air or
  surface-to-air store whose motor has burned out and that drops below
  Mach 1 for a quarter of a second becomes inert: it cannot hit anything and
  falls away. Turning away and running, "dragging" the shot, now works, and
  a shot can be run into the ground.
- **The moment a shot goes dead, the warnings stop.** Your RWR, the incoming
  missile cues, the voice warnings and the AI all forget an inert store in
  the same instant. The missile itself stays visible as it falls.
- **Ranges are fitted, not studied.** Each store's motor is fitted so its
  ranges come close to published reference figures under set launch
  conditions. T.O.R.E is not a study sim; the fits are game approximations,
  listed with their differences in the
  [missile energy baseline](../baselines/missile-energy.md).
- **All-aspect IR stores reach about 63 percent as far head-on as from the
  tail**, at the same target heat, up from 40 percent. Rear-aspect stores
  such as the AIM-9B and AA-2 still need the tail.
- **A missile never hits the aircraft that fired it,** blast and ground
  impact included. Bombs and unguided stores keep their old launch rules.

### Flares and chaff are real decoys

- **A decoyed missile chases the flare or chaff cloud itself.** It cannot
  explode on the decoy, and once it flies past it, it is dead for good. It
  can still hit an aircraft that stays close to the decoy, so release early
  and turn away.
- **One roll per release episode.** Each missile rolls once per decoy type in
  a 2 second window. Extra flares or chaff in that window are spent and drawn
  but do not add to the odds, so spacing your releases beats emptying the
  dispenser.
- **Heat matters for flares.** Afterburner makes you easier to track and
  idle makes you harder; engine off is hardest. A target seen from the side
  or the front shows less exhaust than one seen from behind. Imaging seekers
  strongly resist flares.
- **The notch matters for chaff.** Flying at right angles to a radar missile
  that is looking down at you hides you in the ground clutter and makes chaff
  far more effective. **Chaff against radar stores is 2.6 times as effective
  as the base odds**, but on its own it still rarely saves you: a zero-notch
  AA-12 shot is decoyed about 4 percent of the time per release. The notch
  remains the main defense against active radar stores.

### Range cues show where you can hit

- **The HUD range cue shows the reachable band you are in.** Some stores have
  a gap in their reach, for example a shot that would lob into the ground at
  middle range but hits farther out. The cue now shows the band containing
  the present range, or the nearest one you are closing into, with its own
  minimum and maximum.
- **IN RNG means it.** It shows only when you are inside a reachable band and
  the forecast for the shot as it stands hits. Range and lock cues remain
  advice: you can always fire.
- The AI uses the same forecasts to decide when to fire, and never fires
  into a gap.

## Improvements

### AI missile defense

- **AI pilots defend like pilots.** They roll quickly into full-G breaks up
  to their skill's limit, commit to a beam side and hold the notch, turn
  cold to drag a shot out, and break hard at the last moment.
- **The right device at the right time.** AI pilots release chaff during the
  turn into the beam against a radar missile, cut their heat against an IR
  one, and release in pairs at least 2 seconds apart at every skill level.
  Eight devices cover an encounter, and a pair is kept back for a second
  missile.
- **The AI stops defending as soon as a shot goes dead,** and keeps its plan
  through brief moments when it loses sight of a missile still in flight.
- Novice to Ace pilots differ in reaction time, break timing, device timing
  and how hard they pull.

### Tanks against aircraft

- **Tanks are no longer anti-aircraft snipers.** A tank gun cannot fire at an
  aircraft more than 20 degrees above its hull, waits 3 seconds to lay its
  gun before the first round, and aims wider. In tests a tank's chance of a
  kill on each pass fell by about 70 percent at every skill. A fast jet is
  now almost never hit; a helicopter hovering close by still is.
- Other ground units, and tanks firing at ground targets and ships, are
  unchanged.

### Loading stores in pairs

- **Stores on two-sided points load in even pairs.** Wing, wingtip, shoulder,
  fuselage, tunnel and paired bay points carry matched pairs. A point that
  held 3 now holds 2, and one that held 1 now holds 2. Clicking a station
  adds a pair, and moving stores between points moves a pair or nothing.
- Saved loads are converted: a single becomes a pair, an odd count drops to
  the next even one. A converted load that is now overweight stays editable
  and shows the excess; **Fly** refuses it until it is within takeoff weight.
- In multiplayer, an odd load on a paired point is refused with "Paired
  stores must be loaded in pairs."
- Guided stores now weigh published or class masses, so some loaded aircraft
  weigh a little more or less than before.

### Bridges

- **Bridges stand on their banks.** Decks no longer sit under the ground or
  in the water. Sloping crossings get earth approaches with a gentle grade
  instead of trenches, and the river between the banks stays open.
- **Destroyed bridges look destroyed.** Spans show the original's broken art,
  and bridge ends collapse into ruins. A shot under a suspension bridge passes
  through instead of hitting its shadow.

### Quick Mission placement

- **Objectives are placed to fit each theater.** The creator checks every
  enemy distance against the theater, the ground target and the wings, greys
  out the distances that do not fit and never quietly shortens one. With a
  ground target and a ground start, every distance is offered, because the
  target sets where the enemy starts.
- Ground units stand on slopes up to 20 percent (30 percent for tanks), and
  a unit that cannot stand where it was meant to moves to the nearest good
  spot instead of being dropped. Anything that still cannot be placed is
  shown before launch.
- Ships keep inside the map, carriers and their deck aircraft move onto
  water, and combat air patrol areas stay within the map.
- **Hosts and dedicated servers retry a refused layout.** If a drawn layout
  cannot place every vehicle, the host tries up to 16 further layouts in
  order and sends everyone the one that works. A server mission whose
  `separation-nm` does not fit its theater is refused with "Separation N nm
  does not fit this setup", by `--check`, at startup, in rotation and when
  the King changes the mission. See
  [surface placement seeds](../DEDICATED-SERVER.md#surface-placement-seeds).

### Multiplayer and servers

- **Listed games find the master server again on their own.** A game or
  dedicated server that lost the master tries each of its addresses in turn,
  looks its name up again every minute, and is listed again within
  15 seconds of the master answering, with no restart. This fixes public
  servers that stayed unlisted until restarted, as two did on 2026-10-23.
- **Host handover is steadier.** A player whose game is slow to catch up
  after a new host takes over keeps receiving the game, a standby host no
  longer asks for a fresh copy of the game every 10 seconds, and a takeover
  that takes a few seconds no longer drops every player the moment it
  finishes.
- **Less CPU on Windows and when the game is in the background.** An
  unfocused flight no longer keeps the extra worker threads busy, a window
  with nothing to draw no longer retries in a tight loop, controllers are
  polled less often while the window is unfocused, and a hosting game or
  Windows server no longer spins about a fifth of a core while a mission
  flies.

### Controls and menus

- **Right click turns cycling values back** everywhere: on the controls
  screen (the response curve, the invert box and the setting rows), on the
  Escape menu in flight (Stability level, HUD colour, AI observation rate and
  the on/off rows) and for Internal fuel on the respawn menu. Left click
  still steps forward.
- **The invert box explains itself.** Rest the pointer on an axis row's box
  for a moment to see "INV: invert this axis".

### Slow-frame log

- **Escape > Debug > Slow-frame log** records why frames are slow while you
  fly. Turn it on, play through the slowdown, then send that session's log.
  The log also always records your graphics system at start, and warns when
  the game is running on a software renderer. The
  [README](../../README.md) lists where the logs are kept.

## Fixes

- **Changing anti-aliasing no longer crashes the game.** Switching from Off
  to 2x in **Pref > Graphics...** could stop the game with a renderer error;
  the sun's visibility check now rebuilds with the new setting.
- **A crash now shows its error report.** A failure while a frame was being
  drawn could close the game without the report; it now reports as other
  errors do.
- **Refitting an aircraft as it passes between a player and the AI no longer
  crashes the game**, and its stores are drawn correctly afterwards.
- **A damaged AI aircraft no longer flies into the ground on final
  approach.** Its turning limit was cut so far by damage that it could not
  hold the glide path.
- **The AI no longer runs out of countermeasures in a long fight.** Its own
  missiles in flight kept its defense open forever, so later missiles got no
  devices and no reaction.

## Known limitations

- **0.2.2 and 0.2.1 cannot play together.** Their protocols differ, and so do
  their compatibility codes.
- **Missile ranges are fitted game approximations.** Each store was fitted
  against a few reference launch conditions, and its reach elsewhere is
  measured, not guaranteed. Some stores land outside their reference figures,
  and the [missile energy baseline](../baselines/missile-energy.md) lists
  each difference.
- **Some weak-sustain stores can fly under power below Mach 1,** among them
  the R-27ER and AA-10. They do not go dead until the motor burns out.
- **A head-on AIM-54C shot on the deck reaches far less than one from
  altitude.** Near the ground its path meets the terrain long before its
  energy runs out; climb before a long shot.
- **The range cue can miss a very narrow gap.** It samples the reach rather
  than proving it, and during a hard turn it can stay pending for a moment.
- **Decoy odds, AI defense timing, the tank fire rules and the bridge and
  placement rules are fitted.** None has been compared with the original
  game, and the [feature matrix](../features.md) marks what is opinionated.
- **This release was tested on Linux.** Windows and macOS checks of the new
  missile model have not been run; please report what you find.
- **Some stores stay hidden** from Load Ordnance until they can fly: the
  ADEN, BK27, GAU-13 and GSh-6-23 gun pods and the AA-6, AA-9, PL-7 and PL-10
  missiles.
- Everything under [v0.2.1 known limitations](v0.2.1.md#known-limitations)
  that is not fixed here still applies.
