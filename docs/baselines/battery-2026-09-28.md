This is the full report of the first overnight bug battery and of every change that
came out of it. It covers what was tested, what broke, what was fixed and why, and
what is still open. How the battery works is in [testing](../testing/README.md).
Each lane has its own page with more detail: [menus](../testing/lane-menus.md),
[flight](../testing/lane-flight.md), [ai](../testing/lane-ai.md),
[replay](../testing/lane-replay.md), plus the [ILS findings](../testing/ils.md).

Everything lives on the branch `bug-bash` (161 commits plus 31 merge commits, on top
of `main` at `0e1557a`). It was pushed to `origin` once as a backup, at `9d9fc4b`
(before the combat-speed G fix in section 3.9, item 18); it is pushed again after the
merge to `main`. Nothing else has been pushed.

Provenance labels follow [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md). `fitted` means an approximation
authored because a behaviour is not specified. `opinionated` means a deliberate design
choice; for the changes John asked for on 2026-09-29 it says so, and the numbers are
recorded as agent decisions. `spec-derived` means built from a spec in `docs/spec/`.

## 1. Summary

**What ran.** A battery of 5,105 scenarios across four lanes: 642 menu, 3,269 flight,
727 AI and 467 replay scenarios. (With every one of the 400 seeded AI fuzz cases
switched on the total is 5,445.) 684 of them open a real window. They cover the
menus and the Quick Mission creator, ground start to landing in all 14 aircraft,
AI fights from one against one up to fifteen against fifteen in all 16 theaters,
and mission recording and replay. Each run checks for crashes, impossible numbers
and states that cannot happen. The last complete run of everything, on `9d9fc4b`,
passed 5,104 of 5,105 scenarios in about 85 minutes of wall clock. The one failure was
a real defect in an overnight change, found by the battery and fixed (section 3.9,
item 18).

**What it found.** The player's aircraft, the menus and the recording pipeline held
up well: no crashes, no impossible flight states, no broken art in about a thousand
pictures looked at by eye. The real bugs were in the details around them and in the
AI. The biggest ones a player would notice:

- Stores taken off the Load Ordnance page still showed up and could be selected in
  flight (the "Maverick bug").
- AI aircraft flew into hills, into each other and off the map. A wingman kept
  landing after its leader died.
- A mission ended with an error when an AI dropped flares against some missiles.
- Retail text was read as the wrong character set, so "Berezovka" lost a letter.
- Alt+F4 did nothing on several screens. `--free-flight` never recorded.
- The flight model let aircraft fly 25,000 ft above their ceiling and let loaded
  aircraft sink at full power.

**What John asked for.** After the first reports John made 17 decisions on
2026-09-29 that changed behaviour, and they were built the same day: overspeed shake
and failure, a world edge, gear-up belly slides, smarter AI (terrain look-ahead, leader
succession, traffic avoidance, return to base), the ILS flown by the wheels to a
touchdown zone, AI landings on the same 3 degree path, and stall speeds that rise with
weight. All are in [section 3.9](#39-changes-john-requested-on-2026-09-29).

**Headline results.**

- No crash and no impossible flight state in any lane on the last full run. 5,104 of
  5,105 scenarios passed. The one red scenario was the battery doing its job: it caught
  a defect that the weight-scaled stall speed change had introduced (a fuelled F/A-18D
  could pull only 3.8 to 5.1 G at 450 kt against 7.64 retail). It is fixed, and the fix
  was checked with a targeted set of runs, not a second full battery (section 7).
- The unit and integration tests, fmt, clippy, the Python tests and the doc and asset
  checks all pass on the final tree (2,024 workspace tests).
- The battery now stays in the repository: `python3 tools/battery.py` reruns all of it.

**What John should look at first.**

1. **Weight-scaled stall speeds** (section 3.9, item 18, and section 4). It changes
   every aircraft: liftoff speeds go up 17 to 40 percent, takeoff runs get longer, four
   aircraft no longer lift off inside a 1,074 ft strip, and gear-up at 80 knots now slides
   on the belly. It is `opinionated`. The F-22 family and Su-25 are still 21 to 29 percent
   under the liftoff figures John supplied, because their own data is low.
2. **The AI's short-strip problem.** After the AI lane retuned its speed limits for the
   stall change, all twelve broken AI scenarios pass (section 2). What is left is a
   decision: on the 1,074 ft strips (Santa Fe, San Carlos, Goose Green, Santiago) the
   F/A-18D, Rafale, A-4E, MiG-23 and F/A-XX cannot leave inside the strip, and AI wingmen
   are still placed there and roll off the end.
3. **The seven golden fingerprints** that need new values from the macOS CI log
   (section 5). No recorded value was edited.
4. **The HUD readability decision.** The HUD is hard to read over fog and cloud. A fix was
   tried and reverted because it is a visual design choice (commit `78e62e1`).
5. **The remaining open decisions** in section 6.

## 2. How the battery works and the results

### How it works

`tools/battery.py` runs many `tore-app` runs at once, each on its own copy of an
imported profile, and checks each run's output. A scenario can add its own checks:
text that must appear, files that must be written, or a small function that reads
the output and returns a list of problems. Runs that open a window go through
`tools/agent-run.sh` so they never cover an active workspace. Results land in
`.local/battery/<timestamp>/` (not committed). The runner refuses duplicate names, and
a scenario can be marked as a known failure: it counts as red but expected, and the
run fails once it starts to pass. Full details are in [testing](../testing/README.md).

The battery cannot judge retail parity (there is no retail comparison), how anything
looks or sounds, or other platforms. It checks that the game is self-consistent and
does not break.

### AI lane after the weight-scaled stall speeds

The weight-scaled stall speed (section 3.9, item 18) raised the speeds the AI reads, and twelve AI
scenarios failed with it on and passed with `--retail-stall-speeds`: the landings
`ai-ground-land-selected-wing2`, `ai-rtb-after-win` and the six `ai-theater-*-land-pair` scenarios
(gre, ira, kurile, lfa, ukr, tviet), plus `ai-long-guns-3v3`, `ai-long-15v15`, `ai-fuzz-0028` and
`ai-fuzz-0043`. The AI lane fixed all twelve (commits `4e47944` and `0057684`, merged):

- **Minimum speed.** The AI's minimum was the slowest edge of any envelope row, the 0 G row
  included: 114 kt for a fuelled F/A-18D that needs about 144 kt to hold 1 G, so finals built on it
  sank. It is now never below the level-flight speed at 1 G in the current flap setting (144 kt for
  a fuelled F/A-18D). `fitted`.
- **Maximum speed.** It is now never above the fastest envelope row that still leaves 1.2 G after
  loading. Near the top speed only the 1 G row holds, and the legacy flight model (used by airborne
  AI starts) divides it by the loading, so a loaded fighter at full power sank into flat ground at
  about 1,100 kt. This was the root cause of three fuzz crashes (seeds 43, 183 and 266). `fitted`.
- **Final approach.** The lift margin drops from 1.3 G to 1.15 G (1.3 G asked for more than the
  174 kt final cap allows with the heavier stall speeds), and the flare horizon from 6 s to 4 s (the
  faster finals floated a high F/A-18D past KURILE 3's go-around point). An F/A-18D now flies its
  final at 162 kt. `fitted`, in the [airfield spec](../spec/ai-airfield.md).
- **Earlier landing work, still in place.** `5ecce00` (approach terrain handling at NSK 6 and over
  ridges) and the `ai-ils-terrain-*` scenarios for the seven runway ends the ILS survey flags
  (section 3.9, item 17).

Result: the whole AI lane with all 400 fuzz seeds passed 1,063 of 1,067. The four "failures" were
marker updates: seeds 28, 183 and 266 now pass, so their known-failure markers were removed, and
seed 89 is a new 1,074 ft strip case, so it was marked. Threshold crossings over the whole lane
were 56 to 114 ft up (median 62) at 122 to 169 kt. Mid-air collisions: 11 outside the fuzz seeds,
84 inside them. No AI aircraft in the lane was lost to the new overspeed rule.

**Short strips are still open.** With the heavier liftoff speeds the F/A-18D, Rafale, A-4E, MiG-23
and F/A-XX cannot leave the 1,074 ft strips (Santa Fe, San Carlos, Goose Green, Santiago) inside
the strip. AI wingmen are still placed there and roll off the end, leaving the probe's hazard open.
The lane's known failures cover it (two theater takeoffs, eight fuzz seeds). Whether Quick Mission
should refuse a ground start on a strip too short for the aircraft, or the AI should keep its
wingmen parked, is left for John (section 6).

### 3.9 Changes John requested on 2026-09-29

After the first pass John answered the decision lists on 2026-09-29. Each change was built the
same day. All are `opinionated` (requested by John), and the numbers in each are agent decisions
unless stated. Each entry lists what the player or developer sees now, the reason, the commit
and the check. Items are numbered 1 to 18.

#### Flight and the world

**1. Overspeed shake and airframe failure** (`b497f88`, `9e3b0a4`). `opinionated`, spec:
[overspeed](../spec/overspeed.md).

- Before: aircraft passed their own top speed with no consequence in a dive after a full power
  climb (F-22 1.7 times, Su-25 1.9 times, F-14, MiG-29, Su-27 and X-31 1.5 to 1.6 times). The
  manual (p. 90) says only that the wings will eventually tear off.
- Now: from 95 percent of the top speed at the current altitude the cockpit shakes, rising
  smoothly to a clear maximum at 100 percent (0.024 rad, about 1.4 degrees, two and a half times
  the G-effect shake; player only, view only, repeatable). At 100 percent and above the message
  `OVERSPEED` repeats every 4 s. At 1.5 times the top speed any aircraft, the player's or the
  AI's, is lost through the ordinary structural-failure path with the cause `overspeed` and no
  kill credited. The debrief shows a `Cause` row, the recording carries the cause in the
  destroyed event, and the probe prints `loss: cause=overspeed`.
- **An invulnerable player is immune to the overspeed loss** but keeps the shake and the message
  (`9e3b0a4`). It is not immune to the world edge (item 2).
- Not done: a rumble or sound for the shake (John allowed it as optional).
- Guard: unit tests `an_aircraft_is_lost_at_one_and_a_half_times_its_top_speed` and
  `normal_flight_stays_under_the_overspeed_limits`, the shake test in `g_effects.rs`, and
  scenarios `flight-overspeed-loss-*` (every aircraft lost at 1.6 times its top speed),
  `flight-overspeed-dive-*` (an afterburner dive from 40,000 ft never passes 1.52 times) and
  `flight-overspeed-invulnerable-*`. Normal flight stays well clear: level full afterburner
  settles at 92 to 100 percent of the top speed. In the dive scenario only the Su-25 reaches 1.5
  times; the others hit the ground first at 0.86 to 1.4.

**2. World edge warning at 100 nm and loss at 105 nm** (`b497f88`, `9e3b0a4`). `opinionated`,
spec: [world edge](../spec/world-edge.md).

- Before: aircraft flew 100,000 ft past the map with no boundary message, and AI aircraft with no
  route left the map for good.
- Now: the edge distance is the distance to the nearest point of the map. At 100 nm the player
  sees `You have left the theater: turn back now`, repeated every 10 s. At 105 nm the player is
  lost with the cause `out of bounds`. An AI aircraft is destroyed and despawned at 105 nm with no
  warning and **no kill credited to anyone** (the ledger removes it from credit; overspeed and belly
  losses do the same since `969ef3b`). The loss stays
  fatal for an invulnerable player, so a cheating player cannot fly off forever.
- Guard: the `edge_distance_nm` unit test in `terrain.rs`, the ledger test in `ledger.rs`,
  `lose_out_of_bounds` in `ai_wings.rs`, `flight-edge-*` (fly 200 s out over each edge and stay
  flying) and `flight-edge-lost-*` (lost between 104.5 and 112 nm with the cause `out of
  bounds`). The windowed message path was only compile-checked (section 7).

**3. Gear retraction on the ground, belly slide and braking** (`b497f88`). `opinionated`, spec:
[gear on the ground](../spec/gear-on-the-ground.md).

- Before: pressing G at 80 knots on the runway raised the gear, the aircraft kept rolling on its
  "wheels" and took off gear up, with no message.
- Now: below the aircraft's stall speed on the ground, retracting the gear settles it on its
  belly: a brake of up to 45 ft/s squared (about 1.4 G), the whole aircraft's weight on the
  belly, airframe wear (1 percent of hit points a second plus 6 percent a second at 250 ft/s),
  and the message `Gear up on the ground: belly scraping`. Retraction at rotation speed or
  airborne is unchanged. Hybrid adapter only. Invulnerable spares the player. A wear death
  credits no kill, for the player or the AI (the AI part was fixed later, `969ef3b`, section 3.3).
- With the weight-scaled stall speed (item 18), every aircraft but the F-22 family is below stall
  at 80 knots. An F/A-18D loses 26 percent of its airframe in 75 s; the Su-27 88 percent and the
  Su-25 15 percent; a MiG-29 or X-31 with more thrust than friction wears more than its whole
  airframe and is lost. The F-22 family's thrust carries it through to about 99 knots with 1
  percent wear.
- Guard: unit tests `retracting_the_gear_on_the_ground_slides_on_the_belly_and_wears_the_airframe`,
  `retracting_the_gear_in_the_air_is_normal` and
  `belly_scrape_wears_hit_points_in_whole_points_and_invulnerable_spares_it`;
  `flight-belly-early-*` and `flight-belly-airborne-*` (all 14); the windowed
  `replay-script-takeoff-gear-early`.
- Replaced (John, 2026-09-30): the belly slide from the gear key was replaced by the retail
  ground sensor, which holds the gear down while the wheels carry weight and shows `Ground
  sensor preventing gear retraction` ([roadmap, 1i](../ROADMAP.md#1i-aircraft-pass)).

#### AI behaviour

**4. Speed-scaled AI terrain look-ahead** (`92c6a5a`). `opinionated`, in the
[AI spec](../spec/ai.md#b44-steering-execution-and-pursuit-lead).

- Before: the retail look-ahead was 1,000 ft, which gave a supersonic AI fighter 0.6 s of warning
  before a hillside. This was the "fast low flight" class: F-22, F/A-XX and X-31 crashes into
  hills.
- Now: the floor looks six seconds of travel ahead (never under 1,000 ft), takes the highest
  ground at up to twelve points along that track, and asks at least for the climb that clears it.
  At 440 kt the look-ahead is 4,500 ft; below 100 kt the retail 1,000 ft applies. The six seconds
  is derived from the worst case met (a 90 degree bank rolling level in 2 s, 1 s of pitch
  closure, about 3 s more to raise the flight path 14 degrees at 1,800 ft/s).
- Result: eight AI known failures now pass and lost their markers (F-22N, FA-XX and X-31
  low-level crashes, the long 15 v 15, the F-22 leader's wingman at Krasnodar, fuzz seeds 14 and
  53). The wingman far behind and below its leader now climbs over the hills; the intercept rule
  itself is unchanged.
- Guard: `ai-big-a4e-vs-f22n-researched`, `ai-big-x31-vs-faxx-researched`, `ai-long-15v15`,
  `ai-takeoff-nsk-a5-f22`, `-f22n`, `-faxx`, `ai-known-f22-leader-wingman-ukr3`, fuzz seeds 14, 32
  and 53, `ai-damaged-fault04-hit` and `-gun`.

**5. AI leader succession** (`7366700`). `opinionated`, in the
[AI spec](../spec/ai.md#leader-succession).

- Before: when an AI-led wing's leader was lost, wingmen searched alone forever and flew into each
  other at the leader's last point.
- Now: the surviving member next in the wing's order becomes leader and the others close up behind
  it (members are renumbered from 0, so formation slots follow). A wingman that was following the
  lost leader in to land stops. Human-led wings are untouched. There is no radio call, because the
  retail call needs a living previous leader.
- Guard: the fights and fuzz seeds that lose leaders; the earlier dead-leader rule is
  `ai-regress-wingman-dead-leader`. The lane page does not name a scenario specific to
  succession.
- Later: in the multiplayer work this rule was replaced by multiplayer's lead succession, at
  John's decision of 2026-09-29 ([lead succession](../ARCHITECTURE.md#lead-succession)).

**6. Avoid-traffic rule** (`39e7590`). `opinionated`, in the
[AI spec](../spec/ai.md#traffic-avoidance).

- Before: aircraft flying on their own (two searching leaders on converging courses, leaderless
  wingmen) collided; nothing steered them apart.
- Now: an AI aircraft on its own predicts its closest approach to every other airborne aircraft
  over 6 s. If it would miss by less than 300 ft plus half a second of closing speed (150 ft
  against the aircraft it is attacking), it holds a heading 30 degrees away (to the right when
  head-on within 20 degrees) until 3 s after the last conflict. Missile and gun defense, airfield
  sequences and formation procedures are never overridden.
- Result: over the 126 fight, mission, big-fight and objective scenarios, mid-air collisions went
  from 15 to 2. A 15 v 15 probe runs in the same time (26 s either way). The two that remain are
  where the aircraft were defending against missiles (section 3.3).
- Guard: the collision counter across those 126 scenarios.

**7. AI return to base** (`475cafc`, `51dd2c1`). `opinionated`, in the
[AI spec](../spec/ai.md#return-to-base-when-the-mission-is-over).

- Before: after a fight the winners flew on with no route and eventually left the map.
- Now: once a side has seen hostile aircraft and none is alive, each airborne AI aircraft not
  already landing, recovering or bugged out is ordered to land at its home runway, and its
  wingmen join the leader's landing. A wing with no home runway has its leader fly the
  return-to-base path to its launch point and hold there. Player-led wings stay with the player.
  Nothing changes while any hostile aircraft is alive or any aircraft on that side is still
  waiting or taking off (`51dd2c1`: that hold-back fixed `ai-ground-fight-wing4`, where landing
  traffic kept a wingman from taking off).
- Agent decisions to review: it is triggered by hostile aircraft only, because the AI's objectives
  are air objectives; the "hold" is a figure-of-eight over the launch point (covered by a unit
  test only, since no scenario has a wing without a home runway).
- Guard: `ai-rtb-after-win`, and `ai-rtb-after-win-2v15-ukr`, `-pgu`, `-fra`, `-vla` (fifteen
  winners each; 20 minutes after the start 2 to 5 of the 15 have landed and the rest hold at the
  marshal, one approach at a time).

#### Aircraft performance

**18. Weight-scaled stall speeds** (`9bad0b8`, `41858da`, `a8e9e21`, `21a33e0`). `opinionated`,
requested by John; the reference weight and the rule are agent decisions. Spec:
[takeoff spec](../spec/takeoff-ground-contact.md#weight-scaled-stall-speed). This is a deliberate
departure from the retail polygon data, made because real fighters need markedly more speed to fly
when loaded.

- Before: John doubted the F-22, Su-27 and Su-25 numbers: at 80 knots they were inside their lift
  envelope and he expected 130 to 150 knots to fly. The check (`41858da`) showed the model was doing
  what the imported data said. The sea-level vertex of each 1 G polygon is low (F-22 71 kt, Su-25
  77, Su-27 107, against the F/A-18D's 118.5) and the only weight term was the loading divisor, so
  the stall speed did not change with weight. The F-22 lifted off at 78 kt, the Su-27 at 97 and the
  Su-25 at 73.
- Now: the polygon's slow edges are the aircraft's speeds at its empty weight and grow with the
  square root of the weight (fuel, stores, ordnance), in the hybrid adapter only. The scale applies
  fully to the 0 G, 1 G and 2 G rows, fades linearly to nothing at the 4 G row, and the rows from 4 G up
  stay as imported (`row_scale`, `STALL_SCALE_FADE_G`; the reason is under "A defect the battery
  caught" below). The fast edge, the overspeed rule, the flaps effect (25 percent off) and the
  ceiling rules do not change. The flight
  model, stall warning, autopilot, ejection G check, flight envelope window, belly rule, scripted
  landing probe and crew callouts all read the same scaled speeds. It is the same rule for all 14
  aircraft; nothing is tuned per aircraft. **`--retail-stall-speeds`** (or
  `TORE_RETAIL_STALL_SPEEDS=1`) restores the imported speeds.
- Why the empty weight: trying reference weights of 0.6 to 1.0 times the empty weight against John's
  figures for all twelve types showed no single fraction that brings every aircraft within 10
  percent. 1.0 is the best overall and the one the data supports. `STALL_REFERENCE_WEIGHT_FRACTION`
  in `flight.rs` is the single constant.
- The numbers, and how far each aircraft is from the reference ranges, are in section 4.
- Consequences:
  - Takeoff runs are longer for every aircraft. On the 1,074 ft strips at Santa Fe and San Carlos
    the F/A-18D, Rafale, A-4E and MiG-23 no longer lift off inside the strip (they use the flat
    airport square past its end) and the X-31 barely does. Landing rollouts shorten slightly (an
    F/A-18D stops 3,623 ft past the threshold against 3,956 ft).
  - Gear-up at 80 knots now slides on the belly for every aircraft but the F-22 family (item 3).
    The replay takeoff scripts were retimed for the longer roll (up to 16 s; the A-4E, with no
    afterburner, has a longer script) (`a8e9e21`).
  - Twelve AI scenarios broke and were fixed by retuning the AI's speed limits and final approach.
    See section 2, "AI lane after the weight-scaled stall speeds".
  - The 1,074 ft strips are now too short for five aircraft, for the player as well as the AI
    (section 6, item 3).
- Guard: `flight-liftoff-*`, `flight-liftoff-retail-*` (with `--retail-stall-speeds`) and
  `flight-approach-*` for all 14 aircraft, `flight-takeoff-*` (liftoff follows the model's own
  minimum speed for 1 G), and the retimed `replay-script-takeoff-*`.
- Not changed: the loaded-elevator divisor (see below for why it stays). The HUD landing-speed
  brackets are not implemented in the game, so there was nothing to update.

**A defect the battery caught in this change (`b10e64c`).** The first version (`9bad0b8`) scaled the
slow edge of every G row by the square root of the weight. The loaded-elevator divisor, which
already takes weight off the G an aircraft can pull at any speed, then divided the limit again, so
weight was counted twice. A fuelled F/A-18D at 450 kt and 5,000 ft could pull 5.1 G in a held pull
against 7.64 with the retail speeds, and the replay lane's scripted hard pull
(`replay-script-maneuvers`) peaked at only 3.8 G. The unit tests and the first targeted runs had
missed it. The final full battery on `9d9fc4b` caught it, and it was the only red scenario of 5,105.

- The fix: the scale applies fully to the 0 G, 1 G and 2 G rows (these set the stall, liftoff and
  approach speeds, which do not change), fades linearly to nothing at the 4 G row, and leaves rows
  from 4 G up as imported. Combat-speed G is unchanged from retail: F/A-18D 7.64, Su-27 7.54, F-22
  family 6.91, and every aircraft's held pull equals the imported value. The G limit at the top
  row's own speed is within 2 percent. The weight still costs G below about twice the stall speed,
  where a heavy aircraft really is lift-limited. The flight envelope window follows.
- The divisor stays: removing it would put the F-22's liftoff back near 80 knots (its minimum speed
  for 1 G would drop from 97 kt to about 74 kt).
- New: the `--maneuver gcurve` probe prints the G limit against speed, and the `flight-combatg-*`
  scenarios check the held pull is within 3 percent of the retail value for all 14 aircraft. The
  before and after tables are on the [flight lane page](../testing/lane-flight.md).

14. **Agent decisions in the AI changes for John to review:** look-ahead 6 s, 12 points, 1,000 ft
    minimum; traffic avoidance 6 s, 300 ft plus half a second of closing speed, 150 ft against the
    attacked aircraft, a fixed 30 degree turn, 3 s hold; return to base and its hold pattern; a
    wingman "taking off" counts as on the ground until departure ends; the AI speed limits (never
    below the 1 G level-flight speed, never above the fastest row leaving 1.2 G, final lift margin
    1.15 G, flare horizon 4 s).
    Two choices are open in particular: **leader succession makes no radio call** (the retail call
    needs a living previous leader), and **return to base counts only hostile aircraft**, so a mission
    with none never sends the wing home.
    Later: in the multiplayer work the succession rule was replaced by multiplayer's lead succession,
    at John's decision of 2026-09-29 ([lead succession](../ARCHITECTURE.md#lead-succession)).

**Menus and creator**

15. **The HUD is hard to read over fog and cloud.** HUD green has a luminance contrast of 1.04 to
    1.08 to one against fog and cloud (1.3 over clear sky), so it is told apart by hue alone. A soft
    dark edge fixes it (`78e62e1`) but is a visual design choice.
    John, 2026-09-29: not wanted. The HUD gets no drop shadow, and it stays reverted.
16. **Su-35 wingtip station.** The imported data marks its wingtip AA-11B station as internal, so
    the Load Ordnance page prints `2 (max 2)`, its weight is left out of the payload and Guns only
    strips it. Nobody has checked whether the retail game does the same.
17. **Creator rows that change nothing yet.** The situation row and both nationality rows are
    presentation only.
18. **Runways where a lone aircraft is obstructed.** Greece runway 13 refuses even a single player;
    about a dozen more refuse only a wing of two or more. This is the documented rule, listed in
    case the scenery is wrong.
19. **Ground start on a variant layout with no airports** (developer option only). Nine `~` layouts
    have no imported runway.

**Recording**

20. **Direct `--free-flight` recording.** The fix makes a direct start record. Say if a developer
    launch should stay unrecorded instead.
21. **The airborne flag's meaning.** In a recording `airborne` means "in play" and `on_ground` is
    separate, so a parked aircraft is both. The summary counts only frames off the ground; Tacview
    and the log still write the flag as recorded.

### Needs a human eye or ear

The checks find blank, black and broken frames and impossible numbers. They cannot judge taste,
sound or feel.

- **Sound.** Radio and crew lines, tower voice, seeker and RWR tones, situation music, the stall
  horn, gun and missile sounds, replay sound at 1x, and the volume mixer.
- **Terrain and sky.** Cloudy and foggy weather at the default 5,000 ft start show solid white with
  no ground (consistent with the imported haze tables, not compared with retail); the hour-of-day
  frames (dawn glow at 07, full day 08 to 19, night from 20, so check the sunset transition around
  19 to 20); night desert ground colour (brown, clearly lighter than the sky); white airport
  pavement at Athens and Stanley in daylight.
- **The HUD.** The new BAY label and time rate against the HUD, and the readability decision above.
- **Frames the checks only test for blankness.** Damage, ejection poses, ground starts, the F-22 bay
  doors (hard to see from the capture distance), the twelve replay views, name labels, trails, the
  timeline, the comms panel, the Escape menu pages, and the look of the creator and the debrief.
- **Small things.** The 640 by 900 window (the flight view fills the window while the paused menu
  sits in a centred 4 by 3 box); debug panels overlapping when all are open; `--flight-devices`
  captures that show "RADAR OFF" (diagnostic mode only); whether the Load Ordnance page should print
  an empty station as NOTHING (the manual's wording) rather than an outlined empty box.
- **The AI.** The in-flight activity line during envelope-edge dithering, radio chatter volume in
  10 v 10 and larger fights, how the formation and missile-defense changes look from the cockpit,
  and the 25 second creep at the runway-exit corner where every aircraft leaves the pavement at
  Simferopol (worth a look in the viewer).
- **How flying feels.** The scripts hold a stick position for a time; nothing judges how the
  aircraft responds beyond G, bank, speed and events. The belly slide and the weight-scaled takeoffs
  especially need a hand-flown check.

## 7. What was not run

- **Windows and macOS.** Everything ran on Linux. The macOS golden values are the only cross-platform
  gate, and they are out of date (section 5).
- **Real audio devices and real controllers.** Start-up with sound on and a missing device works;
  profiles were tested as files. No gamepad or joystick was attached.
- **Any comparison with the retail game.** The battery checks that the game is self-consistent. It
  says nothing about parity.
- **The world-edge warning in a real window.** The message path for the 100 nm warning was only
  compile-checked. The loss at 105 nm and the headless path are covered by scenarios.
- **A hand-flown landing.** Scripted pilots land; a person has not. The ILS was checked by survey and
  by the scripted pilot only.
- **AI windowed captures, and the `~` layout variants in the AI lane.** The AI lane is headless and
  covers the 16 base theaters.
- **A second full battery after the last two commits.** The full battery ran on `9d9fc4b` (5,104 of
  5,105, section 2). After it, the combat-speed G fix (`b10e64c`) and the kill-credit fix (`969ef3b`)
  went in, and John chose targeted testing instead of another full run. What was covered: the
  workspace tests (2,024 pass), fmt, clippy, the Python tests and the doc and asset checks; the whole
  flight lane on the G commit (2,415 of 2,415); a 114 scenario AI sample after both commits (114 of
  114); and a targeted subset on the final tree `0048b99` covering `replay-script-maneuvers`,
  `flight-combatg`, overspeed, belly, AI landing, return to base, land pairs, default fights and ground
  start (136 of 136). The menus and replay lanes were not rerun in full after those two commits.
- **Packaging.**
- **The slow missile acceptance set.** `--missile-acceptance` (320 to 480 test shots per aircraft,
  each checked for impossible results) takes about 40 minutes each for the F-14 and Su-35 in a debug

## 8. How to rerun

The steps and the options are in [testing](../testing/README.md). In short, from the repository root:

```sh
cargo build --locked -p tore-app
TORE_DATA_DIR=.local/bugbash-data target/debug/tore-app --import gameassets/fighters-anthology --import-only
python3 tools/battery.py --list
python3 tools/battery.py --lane ai --jobs 8            # one lane
python3 tools/battery.py --scenario 'flight-liftoff-*' # one family
TORE_AI_FUZZ=all python3 tools/battery.py --lane ai    # all 400 fuzz seeds
```

Each lane page lists its own command, its runtime and its known failures. Results land in
`.local/battery/<timestamp>/summary.md`. To rerun the slow missile set:

```sh
CARGO_TARGET_DIR=target-release cargo build --release --locked -p tore-app
TORE_BATTERY_SLOW=1 python3 tools/battery.py --scenario 'flight-slow-*' \
    --bin target-release/release/tore-app --jobs 4
```

Anything that opens a window goes through `tools/agent-run.sh`; the battery does this on its own. To
compare against the retail-data stall speeds, add `--retail-stall-speeds` to a probe or set
`TORE_RETAIL_STALL_SPEEDS=1` for a run.
