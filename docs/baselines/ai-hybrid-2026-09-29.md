Implementation mode, 2026-09-29, measured at `42b3d76` on the `multiplayer`
branch (stage B7 of the [multiplayer plan](../multiplayer-plan.md)). Covers the
mission setting described in [flight model in
multiplayer](../ARCHITECTURE.md#flight-model-in-multiplayer): `standard`, single
player as it is, where AI aircraft fly the legacy model, and `all-hybrid`, every
multiplayer mission, where every AI aircraft flies the hybrid (researched) model
from the first tick.

## In short

The AI fights about the same on both models. Over 168 encounters flown on each,
the same number of enemy aircraft were shot down (260 of 336), one more friendly
AI aircraft was lost on the hybrid model (23 against 22), and the AI fired one
round fewer (911 against 912). No run failed on either model. In any one
encounter the two models never differ by more than one aircraft lost or one
round fired.

This is a check that switching the AI to the hybrid model does not change how
the AI's air combat turns out. It does not compare either model with the retail
game, which is unavailable here.

## What was run

Headless AI probes, 336 in all: 168 encounters, each flown once with the AI on
each model. Every encounter is the default F/A-18D player's wing (the player and
two AI F/A-18s) against two enemy aircraft of one type, 7,200 ticks (60 seconds)
from 10 nautical miles, with the scripted player flying as the probe does.

| Axis | Values |
| --- | --- |
| Enemy type | `a4e f14 f18 f22 f22n faxx mig21 mig23 mig29 rafale su25 su27 su35 x31`, the 14 selectable types |
| Enemy skill | `novice average experienced ace` |
| Geometry | `head side rear`, the enemy's starting heading |
| AI flight model | `standard`, `all-hybrid` |

One case is one command, for example:

```sh
TORE_DATA_DIR="$PWD/.local/dev-profile" target/debug/tore-app --ai-probe-ticks 7200 \
  --separation 10 --probe-enemy-aircraft su27 --probe-enemy-skill ace \
  --probe-geometry head --probe-ai-flight-model all-hybrid --no-audio
```

The player stays on the legacy model in every run (`--probe-flight-model` is not
given), so the only thing that changes between the two runs of an encounter is
the AI aircraft's model. The counts come from each run's output: an aircraft is
lost when its final `actor=` line reads `alive=false`, and the round count is the
`shots=` figure of the `AI probe totals` line. The runner script and the raw
output are local (`.local/mp-notes/hybrid/`), not committed.

## Reading it

- Every encounter's final checksum differs between the two models, as it must:
  the model takes effect from the first tick, so aircraft positions differ by
  small amounts from then on. What matters is the outcome, and of the 168 pairs,
  166 have equal enemy losses, 165 equal friendly losses and 165 equal rounds
  fired. The six pairs that differ each differ by one aircraft or one round:
  enemy losses in `a4e` on the side geometry (experienced 2 / 1, novice 1 / 2),
  a friendly loss and a round in `mig29` (ace, head-on) and `rafale` (ace,
  head-on), a friendly loss in `su35` (ace, head-on) and a round in `su27`
  (average, head-on).
- The timing of the fights is the same: the median and mean time to the first
  enemy loss are equal to the tenth of a second, and the enemies bank a little
  harder on the hybrid model (140.7 degrees against 137.9 on average).
- This supports the decision (John, 2026-09-28, for every multiplayer mission)
  that AI aircraft fly the hybrid model, so a human who takes one over never
  feels its handling change. It shows the AI does not fight measurably better or
  worse for it in these fixtures; it does not show that any single encounter
  plays out alike.

## Limits

- Retail comparison is unavailable, so nothing here says either model matches the
  retail game. Both are the project's own.
- One run for each case: the probe is deterministic, so a second run repeats the
  first, and the spread between runs that a human would add is not measured.
- The player is scripted and does not defend, so the 32 head-on player losses say
  nothing about how a human fares. The friendly AI losses are the wingmen's, and
  all fall in the head-on encounters.
- 60 seconds from 10 nautical miles is one engagement, not a long fight, and the
  player's wing is always the F/A-18D: the other types are met only as the
  opponent.
- The runner's own `summary.csv` has friendly and enemy loss columns that read
  zero, because they count a phrase the probe does not print. The figures here
  come from the `actor=` lines instead.
