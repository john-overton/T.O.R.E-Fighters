# Snapshots at 60 a second near and 4 far (slice D12)

> **T.O.R.E: Tasteful Opinionated Reverse Engineered.**
> The thing being reverse engineered is the *experience*, not the executable. We
> trace what a player does and what the game does back, down to the numbers they
> would notice. How the original code achieved it is history: useful evidence,
> never a blueprint. If a sentence below reads like an instruction to reproduce
> the original's internals, it is out of date.
> <!-- tore-header v2 -->

John asked on 2026-10-06 for the near band (every snapshot) to go from 30 to
60 a second and the far band from twice to 4 times a second, accepting about
double the bandwidth ([decision](../MULTIPLAYER.md#decisions)). Slice D12
(briefed as D11) built it on `mp/d11-rates`. Every figure below was measured
on 2026-10-06 on the development machine (Ryzen 9 7900X, 24 threads, Linux,
release builds, a fresh 1.02F import), **before** with the test binaries
built at the slice's base (`multiplayer` at `5ef1e473`: 30 snapshots a second,
far twice a second) and **after** with the slice's (60 and 4), run back to
back. Other agents' builds shared the machine (load average 3 to 17), so the
host's milliseconds carry a few tenths of noise; the bytes do not depend on
the machine.

## In short

- **Bytes double, not more.** A snapshot packet is the same size at 60 a
  second as at 30 (388 bytes on average on the 15 against 15 mission), so a
  player's download goes from 11.7 to 23.4 KB/s in the packet test and from
  10.7 to 14.9 KB/s to 19.8 to 26.3 KB/s on a whole session. A player's busiest
  second goes from about 41 KB/s to 63 to 74 KB/s.
- **The host's upload** with 15 players goes from 1.50 to 2.63 Mbit/s on
  average (busiest second 4.4 to 8.2 Mbit/s), with 30 players from 3.58 to
  6.31 Mbit/s (busiest second 8.5 to 14.2 Mbit/s).
- **The host's processor** with 30 players goes from 2.5 to 2.6 ms a tick to
  3.4 ms (from 31 to 41 percent of one core); the 99th percentile tick from
  5.5 to 6.1 ms to 7.0 ms, the longest from 7.6 to 9.8 ms to 9.9 to 11.0 ms.
  It still fits: the average is well inside the 8.3 ms a tick, and the few
  ticks over it are run late and caught up, as before.
- **The players' upload** does not change (2.6 to 2.9 KB/s); every limit of the
  netcode matrix still holds, and no frame of another aircraft was drawn past
  its newest update at 60 a second.
- **The standby stream** grows 6 to 9 percent (its ticks go every second tick
  now), well inside the upload need's margin; an observer's stream doubles and
  the observer delay's memory doubles (8.4 to 16.7 MB at 60 seconds).

## One seat's packets

The packet test (slice D6's `tests/bandwidth.rs`): seat 0 on the 15 against
15 mission in the Ukraine theater, 10 nm apart, acknowledged 100 ms later.

| Link | Rate | Packet bytes, mean (max) | Records a snapshot | Snapshots with a due entity waiting, after the first second | Download |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clean, 256 bytes kept for messages | before: 30, far 2 | 388.0 (940) | 36.5 | 352 of 5,400 (6.5%) | 11.7 KB/s |
| | after: 60, far 4 | 387.8 (940) | 36.5 | 699 of 10,800 (6.5%) | 23.4 KB/s |
| | after: 30, far 3.75 | 389.0 (940) | 36.6 | 352 of 5,400 | 11.8 KB/s |
| 5 percent loss, 256 kept | before | 388.4 | 36.5 | 360 | 11.8 KB/s |
| | after, 60 | 388.0 | 36.5 | 705 | 23.4 KB/s |
| Clean, nothing kept | before | 404.4 (1,199) | 37.0 | 6 | 12.2 KB/s |
| | after, 60 | 404.3 (1,199) | 37.0 | 13 | 24.4 KB/s |

A record against a baseline costs what the time since that baseline costs,
and the acknowledgement takes the same 100 ms at either rate (3 snapshots
back at 30, 6 at 60), so a snapshot's size does not change: twice the
snapshots are twice the bytes. The data link's share goes from 271 to 540
bytes a second for the same reason. On this mission nearly everything is
near (within 20 nm or tracked by a friendly sensor), so the far band's
change from 2 to 4 a second moves the records a snapshot by less than 0.1.

matrix, which explains its longest ticks; the first "after" 8-bot run

host never logged a fault or an overload. These costs are far below the

(6.8 ms with 30 then) since the [worker threads](performance-threads.md)
took the AI and the sensors off the host's thread.

round trip and loss cell). Every limit holds before and after, in the short
and the five-minute forms. In the five-minute form: no own-aircraft

drawn within 1 ft in 99.99 to 100 percent of frames at both; frames drawn

## The standby stream and the observer stream

| Run | | Bytes a seat a tick | Warm standby's stream | Cold standby's stream |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crowd fight, 3 humans, 30 s | before | 5.6 | 2.0 KB/s | 7.2 KB/s |
| | after | 6.1 | 2.2 KB/s | 7.4 KB/s |
| 30 humans, PvP, 60 s | before | 3.4 | 12.8 KB/s | 28.9 KB/s |
| | after | 3.6 | 13.2 KB/s | 30.0 KB/s |
| Real 15 v 15, 4 humans, 300 s | before | 4.1 | 2.0 KB/s | 70.6 KB/s |
| | after | 4.4 | 2.1 KB/s | 68.1 KB/s |

The journal's ticks wait for a snapshot interval and go as one record, so at
60 a second each record carries two ticks instead of four and its framing is
shared by fewer; that is the 6 to 9 percent (*agent decision:* kept tied to
the snapshot interval, since the growth is small). Each warm stream stays
under the need host selection asks of it (3 KB/s and 0.52 KB/s a human: 4.6,
18.6 and 5.1 KB/s for these runs). The cold standby's checkpoints are the
mission's and are paced as before (0.56 to 1.09 MB each after, 0.60 to 1.09
before). The "on the wire over a player" figures of the
[standby baseline](standby-stream-2026-10-05.md) subtract one player's whole
traffic from another's, which now doubles, so they are noisier: a warm
standby on the real mission measured 4.1 KB/s before and 5.7 after that way.
Every Check of every warm standby was equal.

An observer of the 30-aircraft crowd fight with a 10-second delay is sent
12.4 KB/s before and 25.0 KB/s after. The delay ring keeps a frame each
snapshot interval: 300 frames for 10 seconds before, 600 after, and for 30
aircraft at a 60-second delay 8.4 MB before and 16.7 MB after (*agent
decision:* kept at the snapshot rate; the test's limit is now 32 MB).

## Host selection: the upload need

Each other player's share of the upload need (`succession::need_per_player`)
was 28 KB/s at 30 snapshots a second. It is now scaled by the game's
snapshot rate: 56 KB/s at 60, 14 KB/s at 15 (*agent decision*: scaled, not
fitted). The measurements above support it: a player's average grew 1.75 to
1.94 times and its busiest second 1.56 to 1.76 times, so twice the old figure
keeps its place between a player's average (19.8 to 26.3 KB/s) and its
busiest second (63 to 74 KB/s), and is at most about an eighth above a
fitted figure. Two players with one warm standby now need 60.0 KB/s; the
throttled-link test (`an_upload_test_on_a_link_at_half_the_need_fails_and_at_the_full_need_passes`)
fails at half of it (51.2 percent arrived) and passes at 1.05 times
(100 percent). The processor test is unchanged: it measures the mission's AI
alone, which the rate does not touch.

## Not run, and what follows

- The real programs on loopback (`tore-server` and `tore-bot` processes) and
  John's machines were not measured for this slice; the in-process figures
  above stand for them, as they did in stage D. The net lane's scenarios ran
  on the new binaries (debug), and their bots report an interpolation delay
  of 54 ms almost throughout, the delay's floor of 50 ms and its half tick,
  as at 30 a second (54 to 57 ms in stage D): on a quiet link 60 a second
  does not draw other aircraft nearer the present, it gives the curve twice
  the points. A lower floor at 60 a second is a possible follow-up.
- **The relay** passed 64 KB/s each way per channel by default when this was
  measured (`relay-rate`, with bursts of 128 KB). A relayed player's busiest
  second is now 63 to 74 KB/s, so in a missile-heavy moment a relayed player
  could lose a few packets for a second; its average (20 to 26 KB/s) is well
  under. John raised the master's default to 128 KB/s on 2026-10-06 (slice
  R1, bursts of 256 KB); a master whose configuration file sets `relay-rate`
  keeps its own value.
- **Captures** of networked flights grow with the download, about twice as
  fast (a player's capture holds every packet).
